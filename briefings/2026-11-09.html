🔐 Custody & Wallet Infrastructure

Exchange breaches, social-login wallets, bridge operators and what attackers are targeting.

Binance attackers had eight weeks inside before the $6.3B exploit, says SEAL

What:The Security Alliance found a multi-stage intrusion with roughly eight weeks of access before funds moved. It sees the same pattern behind other large heists, including the $388M Bitget hack. Protos reports the breach also pushed US officials to have banks check their own systems.
Source:CoinTelegraph · Protos
So what: The keys weren't the weak point. The environment around them was. Put dwell-time scenarios in our threat model: an attacker with admin access to signing infrastructure for weeks. Which alerts fire, who sees them, and can the policy engine stop a transfer even when the person initiating it looks legitimate?

Binance reopens withdrawals; Elliptic says most of the stolen funds haven't been laundered

What:Users pulled a net $3.64B in the first hours after withdrawals reopened, and Binance published a proof-of-reserves report covering 10 major tokens. Elliptic says the attackers swapped tokens for $124M in bitcoin, $2.85B remains frozen, and little has been cashed out. The FBI attributes the hack to North Korea.
Source:Bankless · The Block
So what: $3.6B left in hours, so clients clearly move fast when they lose trust. Make sure our address screening ingests the Binance-hack attribution sets as they're published, because about $3B is still waiting to be laundered and some of it will try to pass through custodians.

Keplr pauses Google and Apple login after unauthorized transactions; Web3Auth says it moved $5.6M out first

What:Keplr shut off social login after users reported drained accounts. Seed-phrase and hardware-wallet accounts look unaffected. Web3Auth says it frontran attackers by sweeping $5.6M from exposed wallets to recovery addresses, though it also mentions an "original attacker," so some funds were likely stolen.
Source:The Defiant · The Block
So what: Social-login key management swaps seed-phrase risk for identity-provider and vendor risk. If we ever offer lighter-weight wallets for end users or treasury sub-accounts, the auth vendor's key-share design and its incident powers belong in the review. Web3Auth could move user funds, and that's worth noticing.

tBTC bridge partly resumes after a Linux kernel flaw; $120M Aave reserve still frozen

What:Threshold halted tBTC on Oct. 30 while operators patched a critical Linux kernel bug that allowed code execution on unpatched servers. The bridge is running onchain again, but the app is still paused and Aave's $119.9M tBTC reserve stays frozen until more operators reconnect.
Source:CoinTelegraph · The Defiant
So what: An OS bug froze a $120M lending reserve for ten days. Ask infra how long it takes us to patch a critical kernel CVE on signer and node hosts, and whether we can rotate hosts without stopping withdrawals.

Mercado Bitcoin said its hack was contained. Onchain data says otherwise

What:Forensic analysis traces $48.6M across 11 chains and shows thousands of customer deposit addresses drained hours after Brazil's largest exchange said it had stopped the breach.
Source:Decrypt
So what: Deposit-address key derivation is a target in its own right. Confirm that our deposit-address keys sit behind the same controls as treasury keys. And if we ever have an incident, our public statement has to match what the chain will show.

💵 Stablecoins & Payments

Issuer charters, issuer economics, card programs and where demand is going.

Bridge wins OCC approval on the path to a federal stablecoin trust bank

What:The conditional order requires $45M in minimum capital and preopening checks. The Defiant notes a bank charter doesn't replace issuer approval under the GENIUS Act.
Source:The Defiant
So what: Bridge is Stripe's stablecoin stack, so a payments giant now has a route to federally chartered custody and issuance. Add Bridge to the competitive matrix next to Ripple and Anchorage, and work out where we'd partner with chartered issuers instead of competing head-on.

Stablecoin growth has stalled, but payments haven't

What:CoinDesk's analysis (the feed carried only the headline) argues stablecoin supply growth has flattened while payment activity keeps growing. Separately, Sky's USDS supply fell to $5.59B and Sky's TVL dropped 15% in a week.
Source:CoinDesk · The Defiant
So what: If balances are flat but flows are up, value shifts from holding stablecoins to moving them. That favors payout, settlement and reconciliation features over yield-on-balance features when we decide what comes next on the roadmap.

Circle beats Q3 estimates on its ARC token sale and cuts 5% of staff

What:Revenue rose 17% to $865M, with $182M from an ARC token presale, while interest income on USDC reserves slipped.
Source:Bankless
So what: Lower rates are squeezing reserve income, so Circle is leaning on Arc. Expect Circle to push harder on Arc settlement with institutions. We should decide whether Arc support belongs on our chain roadmap for next half.

Gnosis Pay breach exposes card numbers, home addresses and linked wallets

What:Attackers accessed the legacy system behind Gnosis Pay's branded cards on Nov. 3. Funds weren't affected and the enterprise card platform wasn't touched.
Source:Decrypt · The Block
So what: Linking wallet addresses to home addresses is a physical-security problem for holders. If we add card or spend features, keep wallet-to-identity mapping out of the card processor and retire legacy systems on a deadline.

Nigeria bans algorithmic stablecoins and introduces issuer licensing

What:The central bank now requires local licenses and reserves for stablecoin issuers and other virtual asset service providers.
Source:CoinTelegraph
So what: One more market where the asset list depends on local licensing. Our per-jurisdiction asset controls need to handle bans by asset type, such as algorithmic stablecoins, as well as by named token.

⚖️ Regulation & Policy

FinCEN, OFAC, Treasury rulemaking and the post-midterm Congress.

Treasury cuts MEXC off from the US financial system

What:FinCEN's order, part of Operation Economic Outcast, bars US institutions from any transfers to or from MEXC, which Treasury says facilitated $16B in illicit finance tied to Iran, North Korea and Russia. OKX is warning that USDT deposits from MEXC may be held for review, and its CEO says OKX treats the order "similar to sanctions."
Source:Decrypt · The Defiant
So what: Treat MEXC as a blocked counterparty for US-nexus clients now. Check vendor tagging of MEXC addresses, set a rule for direct versus indirect exposure, and write the ops runbook for holding and returning inbound funds.

Treasury and the Fed withdraw the 2020 proposals to tighten the travel rule

What:The withdrawn rulemaking would have cut the threshold for collecting and passing on data for cross-border funds transfers from $3,000 to $250. Coin Center says it's the second 2020 surveillance proposal pulled.
Source:The Block · Coin Center
So what: No need to build for a $250 US threshold. Keep $3,000 as the US default while supporting stricter thresholds for EU/TFR clients, where there's no de minimis.

Democrats take Congress; CLARITY Act faces a harder path and Blumenthal sets a Jan. 4 deadline

What:CoinTelegraph expects consumer-protection and ethics demands in any market-structure bill. Bankless frames the next Congress as "investigations, not legislation." Sen. Blumenthal gave Binance, Tether and Trump-linked crypto firms until Jan. 4 to hand over records.
Source:CoinTelegraph · Bankless · The Defiant
So what: Don't plan roadmap items around a CLARITY Act passing in 2027. The GENIUS Act and agency rules are what we can count on. Expect more oversight requests to reach counterparties and vendors, and keep our records ready to produce.

OFAC sanctions eight Canadian money firms over Iran links and lists six Tron addresses

What:The designations freeze covered assets and generally bar US dealings with the firms.
Source:The Defiant
So what: More Tron USDT addresses on the SDN list. Confirm our screening refreshes OFAC lists within hours and re-screens existing whitelisted addresses, not just new transfers.

Protocol upgrades, AI-found vulnerabilities and quantum migration.

OpenAI's automated system found 948 Linux flaws and wrote working exploits

What:The system produced exploits and draft fixes for about $1,279 per flaw.
Source:Decrypt
So what: Put this next to the tBTC kernel incident: finding exploitable bugs in the OS under our signers is getting cheap. Patch speed and host hardening are now product risks, not only infra hygiene. Raise it in the next security roadmap review.

Glamsterdam held off Hoodi as rival proposals try to protect old contracts

What:Developers won't fork the Hoodi testnet until they resolve concerns about eight smart-contract invariants affected by EIP-8037 and EIP-8038. CoinDesk reports two rival proposals aimed at keeping older contracts working.
Source:CoinTelegraph · CoinDesk
So what: Still unresolved two weeks on. Our Safe-style multisigs and any contract wallets with hard-coded gas assumptions are exactly what's at risk. Keep the testnet regression ticket open and follow which proposal wins.

Ethereum is shipping around itself: post-quantum signature checks and faster deposits without a fork

What:Bankless describes faster deposits, cheaper contracts and a post-quantum signature check reaching Ethereum without a hard fork. On Bitcoin, developer Cory Fields proposes making quantum attackers post BTC as collateral before touching vulnerable coins.
Source:Bankless · Decrypt
So what: Post-quantum options are arriving at the contract level before the protocol level. That's a natural follow-on to last week's Coinbase quantum plan, and good input for a one-page quantum readiness position for clients.
