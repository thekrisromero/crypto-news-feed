🔐 Custody & wallet infrastructure

Direct competitor moves, MPC/HSM tech, qualified-custodian licensing, key-management.

Bitget hack hits $387.5M via spoofed internal transfers, not stolen keys

What:An attacker faked internal transfer requests and drained Bitget's hot and warm wallets across several chains in under an hour. CEO Gracy Chen says private keys were not compromised and the fingerprints point to North Korea's Lazarus Group. Withdrawals are paused and the User Protection Fund (over $464M) will cover losses.
Source:Decrypt
So what: Good MPC doesn't help much if the policy engine signs whatever a trusted internal service asks for. Worth a tabletop this sprint: can a compromised internal service in our stack originate a hot-to-warm or warm-to-external move, and which approval would catch it?

Circle and Tether freeze $318K of Bitget loot; THORChain refuses to blacklist

What:The issuers froze about $318K in USDC and USDT, but the attacker had already swapped most funds into ETH. Another $83M moved as XRP that Ripple can't freeze, and THORChain declined Bitget's request to block hacker addresses. One flagged address finished an XRP-to-BTC swap after the request went in.
Source:Decrypt
So what: Issuer freezes recovered less than 0.1% of the loss. Incident response can't depend on third parties. Our screening needs to catch counterparty addresses tied to fresh exploits within hours, including destinations reached through cross-chain swaps.

Legacy Magic Eden approvals exploited; whitehats rescue 23,155 NFTs

What:A flaw in Limit Break's Payment Processor V2 exposed old Magic Eden Ethereum listings. Whitehats secured more than $5.7M in NFTs, and holders still need to revoke the vulnerable approvals.
Source:The Block
So what: Stale approvals are a custody risk that nobody sees. An approvals inventory per vault, with one-click revoke, would be a cheap feature to ship, and clients could show it to their auditors.

Bitcoin's quantum problem moves from theory to logistics

What:A StarkWare, Yukon Research and Eigen Labs contest cut the estimated cost of a quantum-safe Bitcoin transaction from about $320 to $67. The same week brought a new privacy design and a custody playbook for migrating exposed coins.
Source:Decrypt
So what: Institutional clients will start asking how we'd migrate BTC out of exposed address types. We should draft a position paper and track which of our address formats reveal public keys.

💵 Stablecoins & payments

Issuer moves, reserve rules, payment rails and settlement.

Fed proposes GENIUS Act rules: reserves, capital and bank applications

What:The Federal Reserve opened two proposals for comment. Issuers it supervises must fully back tokens with safe assets, face reserve-asset limits and standardized capital charges, and generally have two business days to redeem. Bank applications to issue follow the Act's 120-day decision clock.
Source:Decrypt
So what: A two-day redemption window is an ops requirement for any issuer client, and it lands on us too if we custody reserves or handle mint/burn. Legal should put this docket on its calendar, and product should check whether our redemption flows can meet T+2 at month-end volumes.

⚖️ Regulation & policy

SEC/CFTC/Fed actions, MiCA and EU rules, enforcement.

SEC staff FAQ: token buybacks and liquid staking receipts aren't securities by default

What:New staff guidance says announcing a buyback on a functional network doesn't create an expectation of profit, and qualifying staking receipts fall into non-security categories. One attorney said it makes securities laws look opt-in.
Source:The Block
So what: Liquid staking tokens get easier for US institutions to hold. It's staff guidance, not a rule, so expansion plans for liquid staking support should carry a reversal risk note.

Crypto stopped waiting for Congress after the Clarity Act failed

What:Within days of the Senate failure, the SEC, CFTC and Fed started writing crypto rules themselves. Blockchain Association CEO Summer Mersinger is also stepping down.
Source:Decrypt
So what: Rules written by agencies move faster, and the next administration can reverse them just as fast. Plan features that depend on them so they can be flagged off.

EBA floats stablecoin lending limits and rules for DeFi gateways

What:The European Banking Authority wants the EU to look at rules for DeFi gateways, with leverage caps and certification of lending protocols among the options.
Source:The Defiant
So what: For EU clients, any DeFi access we broker (Aave, Morpho vaults) could need certified-protocol allowlists. Building the allowlist as a policy object now is cheap insurance.

If you're regulated under MiCA, you're also regulated under DORA

Source:OpenZeppelin
