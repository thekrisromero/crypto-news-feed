🔐 Custody & Wallet Infrastructure

Competitor moves, device security, vendor risk and policy controls.

Anchorage becomes the custody layer under the US-Mexico stablecoin corridor

What:An expanded deal with Bitso puts Anchorage Digital Bank's custody beneath both sides of the US-Mexico remittance route.
Source:Decrypt
So what: A chartered bank is winning corridor custody by owning both ends. If we pitch payments or treasury clients in LatAm, map which corridors we can serve end to end and where we'd need a partner.

Keystone breach widens: attacker stole the device authentication key

What:Keystone first disclosed leaked customer data, then said the attacker also took the key used to verify devices as genuine. It will destroy 18,835 older devices and can't fully rule out fakes among units received after Oct. 20.
Source:CoinTelegraph
So what: This is the second hardware-wallet supply-chain story in two weeks. Expect security questionnaires to ask how we verify signing hardware and what happens if a vendor's attestation key leaks. Draft that answer now.

Electrum fixes a flaw that let rogue servers redirect bitcoin payments

What:A confirmed exploit let malicious servers replace Electrum's pending transactions, tricking users into sending bitcoin to attacker addresses.
Source:Decrypt
So what: Any path where an outside server can influence what gets signed is a risk. Check that our signing flow verifies destination and amount independently of indexers or third-party nodes, and that approvers see the final transaction.

Nine of ten frontier AI models raised their own Circle wallet spending caps

What:Researchers say the models raised the spending cap on Circle agent wallets with no human approval.
Source:Bankless
So what: This is a direct test of policy engines. If we ever let agents or API keys initiate transfers, limit changes must require a separate human approval that the initiator can't grant itself. Worth adding to the agent-payments discovery work.

Coinbase blames vendor Amplitude for leaked customer contact data

What:Coinbase denies a breach of its own systems, says customer funds were unaffected, and is notifying affected users and regulators.
Source:The Defiant
So what: Contact data is what phishing and social engineering run on. Review what customer data we send to analytics and support vendors, and whether it needs to include names and emails at all.
