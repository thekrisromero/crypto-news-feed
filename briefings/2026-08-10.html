🔐 Custody & Wallet Infrastructure

Direct competitor moves, MPC/HSM tech, qualified-custodian licensing, key-management.

Coldcard exploit losses top $130M as co-founder deletes posts

What:Losses from the Coldcard hardware wallet exploit passed 2,055 BTC (~$130M) with customers still awaiting a clear root-cause statement. Blockaid's CEO framed it as crypto's "original sin" of raw private keys, and warned crypto is becoming the canary in the coal mine for AI-assisted vulnerability discovery.
Source:Protos · The Block
So what: Expect inbound questions on how we generate and attest entropy and firmware. Publish a plain-English key-generation and signing-device provenance page, and make sure our incident-comms runbook has a named owner and a 24-hour disclosure commitment — the reputational damage here came as much from the silence as the exploit.

BTCPay Server restricts remote Lightning access after node drains

What:A second Bitcoin infrastructure exploit in the same week drained self-hosted merchant Lightning nodes. BTCPay has restricted remote Lightning access while operators patch; total losses and the number of affected merchants are still unknown.
Source:CoinTelegraph · CoinDesk
So what: If we expose any remote-signing or node-management surface, audit it for the same pattern: authenticated remote endpoints that can move funds without a second factor. Worth a targeted review of our hot-wallet operator API this sprint.

Brazil to impose 24-hour hold on transfers to self-custody wallets

What:A new anti-fraud rule effective Jan 1, 2027 will require a 24-hour waiting period on transfers from regulated platforms to self-custody wallets, covering fiat-backed stablecoins as well as crypto.
Source:The Block
So what: This is the first concrete instance of a jurisdiction mandating a time-delay on withdrawal to external addresses — a feature, not just a policy. Our withdrawal pipeline needs configurable per-jurisdiction hold windows and a distinction between whitelisted internal addresses and unknown external ones. Cheap to build now, expensive to retrofit.

Bitcoin and ether ETFs draw $1.1B, best week since April

What:Spot ETFs pulled $1.1B on low trading volume. Bloomberg's Eric Balchunas tied the run directly to the Coldcard exploit, noting several Bitcoin funds saw daily inflows since the hack.
Source:The Block
So what: Self-custody failure translating into third-party custody inflows within days is the single most usable data point in this briefing for pipeline and board conversations. Pull it into the next quarterly narrative.

Bybit sues North Korea and Lazarus Group over $1.5B hack, secures asset freeze

What:Bybit brought civil action against the DPRK and Lazarus Group over the $1.5B breach and obtained an asset freeze — an unusual instance of a victim exchange pursuing state-linked attackers through courts rather than only law enforcement.
Source:CoinDesk
So what: Post-incident asset recovery is becoming a documented playbook. Worth confirming our incident response plan names outside counsel and a chain-analytics partner in advance — freezes depend on speed of tracing and filing.

First quantum attack will look like an unexplained breach, not a headline

What:Executives argue an actor with a cryptography-breaking quantum computer would not announce it by draining famous wallets — the first sign would be a series of key thefts with no explicable cause.
Source:CoinTelegraph
So what: Speculative on timing, but it argues for two concrete things we can do cheaply: address-reuse avoidance by default, and a documented key-rotation and migration path. Both are answers to institutional due-diligence questionnaires that already ask about post-quantum readiness.

💵 Stablecoins & Payments

Issuance, settlement rails, card networks, merchant acceptance, agentic payments.

Western Union and Rain launch a stablecoin card in 37 markets

What:Stablecard holds remittances as USDPT — the Anchorage-issued Solana token Western Union launched in May — and spends the balance anywhere Visa is accepted. The underlying stablecoin has $7.4M in circulation.
Source:The Defiant
So what: A qualified custodian is now the issuance substrate for a consumer payments product at global scale — that is the custody-as-infrastructure business model, and it is a revenue line we should be sizing. Note also that circulation is tiny relative to the distribution footprint: the card came first, the float will follow.

Mastercard extends Crypto Credential to cross-border stablecoin payments

What:A pilot with Borderless.xyz (first participants Infinia, Walapay and Koywe) tests whether compliance checks performed once at origination can be relied on across a network of stablecoin payment providers.
Source:The Defiant
So what: Reusable, portable counterparty attestation is exactly what would let us cut repeat KYC friction on recurring institutional transfers. Track whether Crypto Credential becomes a schema we can consume rather than a Mastercard-only walled garden.

A Tokyo Lawson register takes a 322-yen JPYC stablecoin payment

What:Store-side acceptance ran through Lawson's existing register via HashPort Wallet for Biz and Canal Payment Service's PAYTREE gateway — no dedicated stablecoin terminal added at the till.
Source:The Defiant
So what: Non-USD stablecoins are reaching real merchant checkout without new hardware. Our supported-asset roadmap and NAV/reporting logic should stop assuming USD-denominated stablecoins are the only ones treasury clients will hold.

Cloudflare launches stablecoin wallets for AI agents

What:Account owners hold stablecoins and issue capped Virtual Wallets to their agents, with cloudflare.pay handle reservations opening now and funding, onramps and agent spending arriving in coming months.
Source:The Defiant
So what: The parent-account-plus-capped-sub-wallet pattern is the agentic payments primitive, and it maps almost exactly onto our existing sub-account and policy model. Concrete near-term feature: programmatic sub-wallet creation with per-wallet spend caps, velocity limits and auto-expiry, exposed via API.

Circle names BlackRock, Visa, ICE and DTCC among 11 founding Arc validators

What:Circle's Arc chain will run on a permissioned validator set drawn from the core of TradFi market infrastructure. Circle's own disclaimer notes Arc has not been reviewed by the NYDFS or any other regulator.
Source:The Defiant
So what: A permissioned settlement chain run by DTCC and Visa is a plausible default venue for institutional stablecoin settlement in 2027. Get on the integration radar early, and start the internal debate now on whether we custody assets on permissioned chains and how we handle validator-level censorship risk in our risk disclosures.

Circle expands native USDC to OKX's X Layer

What:X Layer users get Circle-issued native USDC plus CCTP crosschain transfers, continuing USDC's expansion across major chain ecosystems.
Source:CoinTelegraph
So what: Each new native-USDC chain widens the surface where clients expect us to distinguish native from bridged USDC in balances and reporting. If we do not label that distinction explicitly in the UI and API today, it is a support-ticket generator waiting to happen.
