🔐 Custody & Wallet Infrastructure

Key management failures, bridge exploits, MPC/HSM relevance, and signing key security.

Gravity Bridge Drained of $5.4M in Suspected Signing Key Compromise

What:The Cosmos-based bridge was drained of USDC, ETH, USDT, and PAYG tokens after attackers apparently compromised a signing key. Validators halted the bridge mid-investigation; a portion was laundered through ChangeNow and Binance.
Source:The Block  ·  May 30, 2026
So what: Bridge architecture is a custody question — who controls the signing keys and how are they stored? This is a direct competitive talking point: your MPC threshold signing prevents any single key from being a single point of failure. Use this incident in sales conversations this week.

Alephium Bridge Loses $815K to Forged Guardian Messages

What:Alephium's Wormhole-fork TokenBridge was drained on Ethereum and BNB Chain in ~7 minutes after an off-chain backend flaw allowed fraudulent guardian messages to bypass the 4-guardian verification network.
Source:The Defiant  ·  May 31, 2026
So what: Multi-guardian architectures are only as strong as their off-chain validation layer. For any cross-chain features your platform supports, the attack surface includes the message-passing layer, not just key storage. Worth a security review pass if you have any bridge integrations in the roadmap.

StakeDAO Hit by Private Key Compromise, Attacker Mints 5.4 Trillion Tokens

What:A compromised private key let an attacker forge a cross-chain message on Arbitrum, triggering unauthorized minting of vsdCRV tokens. Net loss was ~$91K but the attack surfaced cascading risk warnings across Curve Finance and Beefy Finance integrations.
Source:The Defiant  ·  May 27, 2026
So what: Three key-compromise exploits in one week signals a systemic pattern, not isolated incidents. Institutions watching these headlines are looking for custody providers who can articulate their key management story with specifics — hardware, access controls, audit logs, and insurance.

Mastercard Wins NY BitLicense, Deepening Push Into Stablecoin Settlement

What:Mastercard, which processed nearly $11 trillion in volume last year, secured NY's BitLicense to settle transactions in digital assets. This is one of the most demanding state crypto licenses in the US.
Source:The Defiant  ·  May 27, 2026
So what: When a $11T network gets a BitLicense to settle in stablecoins, it signals the payments industry is embedding crypto rails at scale. This is both a competitive signal (Mastercard is becoming a settlement infrastructure player) and a distribution opportunity — enterprise clients using Mastercard networks will expect their custodian to integrate with those rails.

Circle Court-Ordered Freeze Traps $12.6M in Zama's cUSDC — Innocent Depositors Locked Out

What:A court-ordered restraining order in the Overnight Finance lawsuit caused Circle to blacklist Zama's confidential USDC wrapper contract. The privacy protocol isn't a defendant, but every depositor in the pooled contract is now locked out alongside the targeted funds ($12.6M frozen).
Source:The Defiant  ·  May 30, 2026
So what: This is the clearest real-world demonstration of USDC's centralized freeze risk to date — and it affected uninvolved users in a pooled contract. Your platform should explicitly document this risk for clients holding USDC in custody, and consider whether your product surfaces any pooled-asset exposure that could create similar collateral freezes.

Fed and Bank of England Clash Publicly on Stablecoin Viability

What:Federal Reserve governor Christopher Waller said stablecoins expand the reach of US monetary policy; Bank of England's Megan Greene countered that their popularity will soon fade. Divergent G7 central bank views signal no near-term global framework consensus.
Source:CoinTelegraph  ·  May 31, 2026
So what: If your clients operate across jurisdictions, this regulatory fragmentation matters for how they hold and settle in stablecoins. The US is bullish; the UK is skeptical. Build your compliance disclosure to be jurisdiction-aware for stablecoin exposure.

Dimon Escalates Battle Against Stablecoin Yield in CLARITY Act Debate

What:JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon is publicly lobbying against provisions in the CLARITY Act that would allow stablecoin issuers to pay yield to holders, arguing banks "will not accept it."
Source:CoinDesk  ·  May 29, 2026
So what: The outcome of the yield-bearing stablecoin debate shapes the entire business model of stablecoin custody — whether clients will demand yield pass-through and whether your platform needs to support it. Watch the CLARITY Act closely; this is a potential product requirement trigger.
