🔐 Custody & Wallet Infrastructure

Direct competitor moves, MPC/HSM tech, qualified-custodian licensing, key-management.

Coldcard ships firmware fix after $130M bitcoin exploit, now requires user-supplied entropy

What:Coinkite's latest firmware makes users add their own randomness when generating wallet seeds, and patches further issues found during a three-week review. CoinDesk reports the vendor used AI-assisted review to surface additional bugs.
Source:Decrypt · CoinDesk
So what: This is an entropy-quality failure, not a key-theft failure, and our diligence questionnaire for hardware signers does not currently ask how seeds are generated or whether the RNG is attestable. Add an entropy-provenance question to vendor review, and check whether any customer-held signing devices in our supported set share the affected generation path.

Anchorage launches agentic banking platform with a "know your agent" setup

What:Anchorage's CEO says AI agents need their own bank accounts, and the firm has built a platform with agent-level identity checks to support machine-initiated transactions.
Source:The Block
So what: A federally chartered competitor just staked a claim on non-human account holders. Our roles-and-approvals model has no principal type for a service identity with its own spend limits and revocation path, and retrofitting that into the policy engine is a quarter of work, not a sprint. Start the design doc now.

OpenZeppelin: institutions are building their own onchain identity and access-control layer

What:A protocol sees every address as a string with a balance. There is no native field for verified investor or sanctioned entity, so institutions working with tokenized assets are building that layer in contract logic instead.
Source:OpenZeppelin
So what: If tokenized assets increasingly enforce allowlists at the contract level, a custody wallet that is not on the allowlist cannot receive the asset at all. We should be able to register client wallet addresses with issuer identity registries as part of onboarding, and surface allowlist status in the asset support matrix.

Arbitrum activates Elara, giving chain owners optional compliance screening

What:On dedicated Arbitrum chains, the chain owner rather than ArbitrumDAO controls transaction screening. Priority fees stay off on Arbitrum One pending a separate vote.
Source:The Defiant
So what: Compliance-filtered app chains are a live deployment target for bank clients, which means "which chain is this asset on" becomes a control question rather than an integration detail. Our chain-support docs should note screening policy per network so sales can answer it without escalating.

TRM Labs: AI use in crypto crime up 40% year over year

What:Attackers are using AI to find overlooked vulnerabilities and to infiltrate IT service firms, per TRM's latest report.
Source:The Block
So what: The IT-vendor infiltration angle is the one that touches us directly: it is a supply-chain and social-engineering path into approval workflows, not a cryptography problem. Push for out-of-band verification on any approver-detail change, and get this into the next security review.

💵 Stablecoins & Payments

Issuance, licensing, settlement rails, card and payout flows.

Treasury proposes GENIUS Act licensing rules with a due-diligence duty on listing venues

What:Treasury's proposal would require exchanges that list stablecoins to run due diligence on them, extending obligation past the issuer to the distribution layer.
Source:Bankless
So what: The highest-signal item this week. We are not an exchange, but "platform that makes a stablecoin available to clients" is close enough that counsel should read this and tell us where we land. If it applies, we need a documented, versioned diligence record per supported stablecoin and a UI surface that shows clients which assets carry issuer attestations.

Deel takes its DLUSD stablecoin wallet to 80+ countries

What:The payroll platform's dollar balance is issued via Stripe's Bridge and settles on Tempo, now live across Latin America, Africa, the Middle East and Asia-Pacific. The US, UK, EU and Australia are excluded.
Source:The Defiant
So what: A branded, single-purpose dollar token settling on a rail we may not support. Expect treasury clients with international contractors to ask us to hold and reconcile balances like this. The product question is whether we support long-tail issuer tokens generically or maintain a curated list, and that decision is easier to make now than after the first customer request.

Crypto card spending passes $1 billion

What:CoinDesk reports crypto-linked card volume crossing the $1B mark, with stablecoins moving into ordinary purchases rather than trading-adjacent use.
Source:CoinDesk
So what: Card spend means many small debits from a corporate balance, which is a different traffic shape than the large scheduled transfers our approval flows are tuned for. If we want to sit behind a spend program we need policy tiers that auto-approve under a threshold, plus reporting that rolls thousands of small outflows into something a controller can reconcile.

AI agents pitched as the next wave of stablecoin users

Tokenized deposits and funds, corporate balance sheets, institutional settlement.

HSBC and Standard Chartered complete first live tokenized deposit transfer on Swift's blockchain ledger

What:Most tokenized deposits today run on single-bank chains and only work between customers of that bank. Swift's ledger matched and netted obligations recorded on the two banks' separate platforms, though final settlement still went through existing systems.
Source:Ledger Insights · The Defiant
So what: Tokenized bank deposits are the most credible competitor to stablecoins for corporate treasury, and Swift interoperability removes their biggest limitation. A treasury dashboard that only shows public-chain assets will look incomplete to a CFO holding both. Worth a discovery conversation about whether we aggregate tokenized deposit positions read-only before anyone asks.

Shinhan partners with Solana Foundation, Etherfuse and Orca on a won-denominated tokenized fund

What:The agreement covers a proof-of-concept for issuing and distributing a Korean won tokenized fund.
Source:The Block
So what: Non-dollar tokenized money-market instruments are showing up from major banks, which stresses our asset model in a specific way: multi-currency NAV, yield accrual, and redemption windows rather than simple token balances. If our data model treats every holding as a spot token, tokenized funds will not report correctly.

Tokenized equities push forward, with a warning about settlement plumbing

That is a positioning argument for our reporting and attestation work, and cheap to add to the narrative now.

SEC proposes a "Regulation Crypto Assets" framework with a $75M exemption

What:The proposal pairs two registration exemptions with a conditional safe harbor that would remove the investment-contract label from a token, and preempts state securities law. All three sitting commissioners backed it in a vote held outside a public meeting.
Source:The Defiant · Bankless

If it lands roughly as proposed, our asset-listing pipeline gets faster and the bottleneck moves from legal review to engineering integration. Ask counsel for a read on how the safe-harbor conditions map to our current listing checklist.

CFTC chair says the agency will act on crypto rules if the CLARITY Act fails

Source:CoinTelegraph

⛓️ Smart Contracts & Infrastructure

Chain-level changes, bridge and governance failures, audits.

The Sandbox contains bridge exploit that minted unbacked SAND on Base and BSC

What:Upbit and Bithumb froze SAND transfers under South Korea's user-protection law. Upbit halted deposits and withdrawals on Ethereum, the chain the studio says was not affected.
Source:The Defiant · CoinDesk
So what: Two lessons. First, a client holding a bridged asset can be holding an unbacked token while our balance display shows it as fine, so supply-invariant monitoring on bridged assets is a real gap. Second, the exchanges froze the unaffected chain too, which means our runbook should assume conservative blanket freezes and tell clients what is actually withdrawable during an incident.

MANTRA halts its chain and points at the Cosmos EVM module

What:The Dubai-licensed RWA Layer 1 has been frozen since Thursday evening. MANTRA says the incident touched two wallets it controls and no user funds were taken, and has not said whether anything left those wallets.
Source:The Defiant · The Block
So what: A licensed RWA chain going dark is the tail risk clients ask about when we pitch tokenized assets, and a shared Cosmos EVM module means the blast radius may extend past one chain. Our chain risk tiering should account for shared-dependency correlation, and we need a defined client-facing status for assets on a halted network. "Balance shown, transfers unavailable, indefinite" is not currently a state our UI can express.

Term Finance loses about $8.5M in a vault governance exploit

What:Term permanently closed its Meta Vaults after an attack reportedly removed nearly all of their Ethereum deposits.
Source:CoinTelegraph · The Block
So what: Governance was the attack surface, not the contract math, which is exactly the failure mode audits miss. Any protocol we expose through a yield or DeFi feature needs governance-parameter review in the integration checklist: who can change vault config, over what timelock, and do we alert clients when those params move.

Solana cuts mainnet slot time to 350ms, targeting 200ms

What:Faster confirmations, though the change is not designed to raise overall network throughput.
Source:The Block · The Defiant
So what: Our Solana confirmation depth is probably specified in slots, so a shorter slot means less wall-clock finality for the same setting. Worth an engineering check that our confirmation policy is time-based or was re-derived after this change, and the same review applies to any indexer assuming fixed slot timing.
