🔐 Custody & Wallet Infrastructure

Direct competitor moves, MPC/HSM tech, qualified-custodian licensing, key management.

BitGo posts $19M Q2 net loss and loses its CFO, even as revenue climbs 80%

What:Revenue jumped 80% year over year but trading and staking margins weakened; BitGo is targeting $15 million in annualized savings after June layoffs, and the CFO is departing. Separately, Mizuho cut its price target to $11 while calling the business high-growth and recurring-revenue, with a 27% year-over-year increase in customer count.
Source:The Defiant · The Block
So what: Our closest listed comparable is proving that custody revenue growth does not automatically carry margin. Pull our own unit economics on trading and staking spread versus pure custody fees before the next pricing review, and use the customer-count growth number as the benchmark our board will anchor on.

OCC grants World Liberty a conditional national trust charter, pulling USD1 issuance away from BitGo

What:World Liberty Financial received preliminary OCC approval to become a national trust bank. The proposed World Liberty Trust Company would take over issuance of the USD1 stablecoin from BitGo, which currently handles it.
Source:The Block · Decrypt
So what: Two lessons. First, the OCC national trust charter is now a live and reasonably fast route to qualified-custodian status — worth a real cost-and-timeline estimate rather than a footnote. Second, issuer clients will in-source custody once they hold their own charter, so our stablecoin-reserve custody revenue should be modelled as a stepping stone, not an annuity.

Galaxy estimates Coldcard-related Bitcoin thefts could exceed $150 million

What:Thefts tied to compromised Coldcard hardware wallets have slowed, which Galaxy Research reads as vulnerable holders having either migrated or already been drained. The week's broader self-custody reckoning included a single $116 million wallet exploit.
Source:Decrypt · CoinTelegraph
So what: This is the strongest self-custody-to-qualified-custody migration narrative we have had in a while. Ask marketing for a one-pager comparing our key-generation ceremony, attestation, and insurance posture against a consumer hardware wallet, and make sure sales has it before the next enterprise cycle.

Trezor and SafePal breaches expose roughly 54,000 wallet customers

What:A breach at Trezor's shipping partner ShipMonk exposed names and contact details for 13,689 customers across seven countries who ordered between May 10 and August 8; Trezor says its devices and systems were not compromised. Separately, SafePal disclosed a breach affecting nearly 40,000 customers' order information, with phishing reports dating to July.
Source:The Defiant · The Block
So what: Neither breach touched a key — both touched a vendor. Our third-party risk register should get a fresh pass on fulfilment, CRM, support desk, and marketing-automation vendors, since a client list leak from any of them creates targeted phishing against exactly the accounts with the largest balances.

Bank Leumi will offer crypto trading on Galaxy's custody stack from early 2027

What:Israel's largest bank is retrying crypto after a 2022 attempt died at the Bank of Israel. Customers will buy, hold, and sell Bitcoin, Ether, and Solana directly in the bank's investment app, with Galaxy supplying custody. Israel saw an estimated $22 billion in onchain value in the twelve months to June 2025, per Chainalysis.
Source:Decrypt · CoinTelegraph
So what: The bank-distribution deal is becoming the standard custody go-to-market, and Galaxy is winning them. Worth checking whether our sub-custody API surface — asset onboarding, per-customer segregation reporting, and local regulatory reporting hooks — is actually ready to be white-labelled by a tier-one bank, or whether we would need a six-month integration to say yes.

💵 Stablecoins & Payments

Issuance, reserves, yield, card rails, and cross-border settlement.

Tether clears its first full KPMG audit — to AICPA standards, without publishing the statements

What:KPMG issued an unqualified opinion on 2025 accounts for Tether's El Salvador issuing entity. The work was performed to AICPA standards rather than the PCAOB standards the GENIUS Act sets for large issuers, and Tether has not published the statements, citing its status as a private company. The CEO dismissed critics.
Source:The Defiant · The Block
So what: The AICPA-versus-PCAOB gap is the detail that matters for us. If our platform surfaces reserve attestations to treasury clients, we should label the standard each issuer was audited against rather than showing a generic green tick — that distinction is exactly what a client's own auditor will ask about.

Standard Chartered's Anchorpoint launches HKDAP stablecoin beta with HashKey and OSL

What:Anchorpoint, Standard Chartered's Hong Kong stablecoin venture, opened the beta phase of HKDAP issuance alongside licensed Hong Kong exchanges HashKey and OSL, following the Hong Kong Monetary Authority's licensing regime.
Source:Ledger Insights
So what: Non-dollar bank-issued stablecoins are moving from announcement to live issuance. If our asset-listing pipeline is still dollar-first, HKD and other licensed regional units need a place in the queue — APAC treasury clients will want to hold settlement balances in their own currency, not just in USD.

Tempo launches embedded stablecoin yield through Morpho vaults and tokenized money funds, starting with Deel

What:Tempo Earn routes rewards through Morpho vaults and tokenized money market funds, deliberately placing the yield outside the stablecoin issuer, which US law bars from paying interest. Deel's contractor wallet is the launch partner.
Source:The Defiant
So what: This is the emerging template for legally paying yield on stablecoin balances: keep the issuer out of the flow and route through a vault or tokenized fund. Our treasury product needs a view on whether we intermediate that structure, custody the vault positions, or stay out — clients will ask, and 'the issuer can't pay you' is not a sufficient answer anymore.

The stablecoin yield fight between banks and crypto is not going away

What:Banks and crypto firms remain locked in a fight over whether stablecoin holders should earn yield, with traditional deposit economics on one side and the industry's growth case on the other. It is one of the unresolved issues Galaxy cited in cutting CLARITY Act odds.
Source:CoinDesk
So what: The outcome directly sets the ceiling on our treasury product's headline rate. Build the roadmap so the yield source is pluggable — issuer rebate, tokenized money fund, or vault — because the legally permitted route is likely to change at least once before this is settled.

Rain acquires merchant wallet startup Ansa

What:Stablecoin card issuer Rain bought Ansa, which builds branded prepaid wallets holding dollar balances for merchants with no stablecoin leg. Founder Sophia Goldberg joins Rain as head of payments; terms were not disclosed.
Source:The Defiant
So what: Stablecoin card issuers are buying their way into stored-value balances, which means they will accumulate float that needs custody and reconciliation. That is a segment we do not usually pitch — worth a quick sizing exercise on stablecoin card programs as a custody buyer persona.

🏦 Treasury, RWA & Corporate Adoption

Tokenized funds and collateral, corporate treasuries, institutional allocation.

SEC staff clears Franklin funds to use an onchain money fund for cash and collateral, under twelve conditions

What:The relief lets Franklin Templeton's registered funds hold FOBXX through an affiliated blockchain-integrated system, subject to twelve custody and control conditions.
Source:The Defiant
So what: This is the single most product-relevant item of the week. Those twelve conditions are effectively a draft spec for what SEC staff considers adequate control over tokenized fund shares. Someone on our side should read them line by line and map each to a control we already have, a control we can build, or a gap — that map is the fastest route to a defensible tokenized-collateral offering.

SEC advances a tokenized-securities exemption that could enable 24/7 trading

What:Chair Atkins says the framework would support compliant onchain trading while longer-term rules are developed. A separate offering proposal was on Friday's Commission agenda — before the Commission shelved the meeting.
Source:The Defiant
So what: Continuous trading breaks the assumptions in most custody operating models: cut-off times, end-of-day reconciliation, and settlement-window staffing all assume markets close. Worth an early engineering spike on what a 24/7 settlement and reconciliation posture would actually cost us.

Securitize hits $4.3 billion in tokenized assets while revenue falls 12% and costs rise 56%

What:Tokenization revenue dropped 12% and operating costs jumped 56%, showing that higher onchain assets under administration have not yet produced steadier earnings.
Source:The Defiant
So what: A useful reality check for anyone in our org modelling tokenization as a high-margin adjacency. Assets under administration are growing faster than the ability to monetize them, which argues for us monetizing tokenized assets as custody and collateral servicing rather than as issuance.

Tokenized stock holders more than double as monthly volume surges 179%

What:Tokenized equities reached 1.31 million holders over the past month, with monthly transfer volume up 179% to $23.13 billion and distributed value up 5.9% to $2.38 billion. Tokenization-linked stocks slipped on the SEC meeting delay.
Source:CoinTelegraph
So what: Transfer volume growing far faster than holder value suggests high-velocity trading rather than buy-and-hold. If we custody tokenized equities, our fee model should assume transaction-heavy usage, and our reconciliation load will scale with transfers, not with balances.

Norway's sovereign wealth fund hits record indirect Bitcoin exposure, adds $81.9M BitMine stake

What:Norges Bank Investment Management's indirect Bitcoin exposure reached an all-time high, with Strategy accounting for 86% of it, per K33. It also disclosed 6.15 million BitMine shares worth about $82 million at June 30, giving it indirect Ethereum exposure.
Source:The Block · The Defiant
So what: The largest allocators are still getting exposure through listed proxies rather than direct custody. That is our addressable gap: the pitch is not 'own crypto', it is 'replace 86% single-name concentration risk with a directly custodied position'. Concentration risk is the wedge.

MSCI proposal could remove Strategy and Metaplanet from global indexes

What:An MSCI proposal introduces a non-operating company screen that could exclude digital-asset treasury companies from its global indexes. Strategy responded that MSCI should measure markets, not dictate corporate asset allocation.
Source:The Block · CoinDesk

Bitwise explores tokenizing its Solana staking ETF via Superstate

What:Shares held in tokenized form would carry the same rights as book-entry shares, though they would not be freely transferable outside the system.
Source:The Block
So what: A tokenized ETF share that is restricted to a permissioned transfer system is a custody product, not a DeFi one. If this pattern spreads, we need to support holding tokenized fund shares whose transferability is gated by the transfer agent — a different model from freely transferable tokens and worth confirming our ledger can represent.

Source:Decrypt

What:Ireland's first AML strategy of its kind introduces enhanced due diligence for transfers involving private wallets and for firms dealing with overseas crypto companies, alongside proposed industry standards for illicit-use detection.
Source:Decrypt · CoinTelegraph
So what: Self-hosted wallet withdrawals are getting screened more tightly across the EU. Our withdrawal flow should already support proof-of-address-ownership and counterparty attestation per jurisdiction; if it is a manual compliance step today, that is a scaling problem worth a ticket.

Source:Protos
So what: Deposit-crediting logic that trusts an unverified source-chain event is the same failure mode that hits custodians, not just bridges. Worth confirming our deposit finality rules require independent confirmation depth per chain rather than relying on a single indexer or node provider.

So what: One hosting provider's routing failure came close to stalling finality on a chain we custody and stake. Our staking infrastructure should be checked for the same concentration — if our validators sit with one or two providers or in one region, that is a client-facing availability risk we should be able to answer for in a due-diligence questionnaire.

Ethereum devs narrow 66 proposals for the Hegotá upgrade, aiming at native privacy

What:Core developers are working to reduce the 66 proposals attached to the Hegotá upgrade, which aims to bring more native privacy to Ethereum applications.
Source:CoinTelegraph
So what: Native privacy at the protocol level will complicate transaction monitoring, travel-rule attribution, and proof-of-reserve visibility. Better to get our compliance and engineering leads reading the shortlist now than to discover post-fork that a screening vendor cannot see what it used to.

Circle's cirBTC goes live on Ethereum with only 40 BTC outstanding

What:Circle National Trust custodies the backing, with published Bitcoin addresses and Chainlink Proof of Reserve providing onchain visibility. Support on Circle's Arc chain is still forthcoming.

If we custody wrapped or bridged assets, clients will increasingly ask for that same triple — worth checking what we can produce today without engineering work.
