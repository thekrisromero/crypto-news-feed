What:RLUSD went live in Japan after FSA approval, becoming the first foreign-issued stablecoin classified under Japan's revised Payment Services Act — with Circle and Nomura moving on the same market.
Source:The Defiant
So what: Jurisdiction-specific stablecoin approvals mean our treasury clients will increasingly want to hold and settle in regional regulated coins. We should scope RLUSD support and a framework for onboarding new jurisdiction-approved stablecoins quickly.

MoneyGram CEO on rolling out MGUSD to 60M users globally

Source:The Defiant
So what: A payments incumbent issuing its own coin and running a validator is exactly the enterprise treasury customer we want. Signals demand for custody + settlement of branded/partner stablecoins — a potential B2B2C wedge for us.

BIS warns stablecoins risk fragmenting the global financial system

What:In its annual report the Basel-based BIS said private digital tokens fall short of the requirements for sound money and urged policymakers to accelerate tokenized central- and commercial-bank money instead.
Source:CoinTelegraph
So what: The BIS stance foreshadows tighter reserve/redemption rules and a possible tokenized-deposit competitor. We should design custody to be issuer-agnostic and ready to support tokenized bank money, not bet the roadmap on any single stablecoin model.

So what: Real demand is in LatAm, Africa, and SE Asia. If our custody/treasury product targets only US/EU institutions we miss where volume actually lives — worth a look at emerging-market treasury and FX-corridor use cases.
