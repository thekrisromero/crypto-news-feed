Welcome to your weekly Crypto Custody and Treasury briefing for the week of September twenty-first, twenty twenty-six. This week the news feed pulled one hundred forty-eight stories across ten active sources, and I picked twenty of them. There is one story underneath almost all of them, so let me start there.

The CLARITY Act died in the Senate, and within about seventy-two hours the agencies had picked up the pen. That is the shape of the week. Congress stepped back, and the S E C and the C F T C stepped forward with an appetite that is honestly a little startling.

Here are the top three takeaways.

First takeaway. The S E C's rewrite of the custody rules for investment advisers and investment companies is now sitting at White House review. That is the O M B stage, which means the text is drafted and close to public. It replaces the safeguarding proposal the commission withdrew back in twenty twenty-three. So what for us: our compliance roadmap should stop planning around a statute and start planning around a rule. Practically, that means comment letter deadlines and implementation clocks measured in months rather than a two year legislative wait. It also means whatever we get can be undone by the next commission chair, which is a different kind of risk than we have been planning for. Somebody on our side should be watching the O M B docket weekly starting now.

Second takeaway. Charters are becoming the moat, not technology. Bastion won conditional O C C approval for a national trust bank that will offer stablecoin custody, wallets, payment infrastructure, and white label issuance all out of one federally regulated entity. Meanwhile, in Europe, banks now make up twenty-three percent of the M i C A provider register, which is roughly double their share in late June. So what for us: our competitive set is shifting away from crypto native custodians and toward single entity regulated stacks. My guess is that every deal we lose this year will cite one regulated counterparty as the reason. That is a positioning problem more than a feature problem.

Third takeaway. Tokenized equity went from a legal question to a collateral asset in under a week. The S E C issued a five year conditional exemption letting tokenized national market system stock trade on permissioned automated market makers, with full shareholder rights attached. Days later, Morpho opened U S D C borrowing against five Coinbase stock tokens on Base. So what for us: custody for a rights bearing token is a fundamentally different product than custody for a payment token. Dividends, stock splits, proxy voting, cost basis and tax lot reporting all land on our side of the line. This is a roadmap item now, not a research spike for next year.

Now let me walk the sections.

Starting with custody and wallet infrastructure.

The Bastion charter is the headline. Bastion Platforms National Trust Company gets to do stablecoin custody, wallets, payments and white label issuance from one federally regulated entity. That is the exact bundle we compete against in enterprise deals. I would pull the conditional approval letter itself, because the conditions the O C C attached are effectively a map of what a trust charter cannot do. That map is more useful to us than the press release.

The S E C custody rewrite is the single most consequential item in this whole briefing. Whatever definition of qualified custodian survives review, and however the rule handles the phrase possession or control, becomes our registered investment adviser sales script and the scope of our attestations. We should be drafting our comment letter before the text lands rather than scrambling inside a two week window.

Third in this section, the C F T C extended the relief it gave to Phantom wallet to the whole industry. Staff opened introducing broker relief to software developers generally, so every non custodial wallet and DeFi interface now sits under the same posture. That lowers the regulatory cost of shipping a self custody or M P C wallet that routes out to third party venues, which is exactly what treasury clients keep asking us for. One caution: this is staff level relief and it can be withdrawn, so anything we build on top of it should survive that withdrawal.

Fourth, the European picture. Banks are now nearly one in four providers on the E S M A M i C A register, roughly double where they were at the end of June. In Europe the incumbent banks are the competition now, not the distribution channel. If our European go to market still assumes banks need us in order to reach crypto, that assumption is about six months out of date. The pitch that still works over there is sub custody and white label.

And fifth, a strange one. The Wall Street Journal reported that E C B president Christine Lagarde personally intervened to block Binance's M i C A license application in Greece. Binance called the reporting speculation and reaffirmed its commitment to European authorization. M i C A passporting is supposed to be a technical process, and this suggests it is partly political. If we are choosing a home state regulator, we should weight E C B relationships alongside processing speed. The fast jurisdiction is not automatically the safe one.

Moving to stablecoins and payments.

Circle launched its own Layer One. Arc mainnet is live, joining chains already operated by Tether and Stripe. Every major issuer now runs a chain. For us that means our chain support matrix grows by three, and it means clients will ask why their U S D C balance on Arc looks different from their U S D C balance on Base. I would treat issuer native chains as a first class integration tier and get in front of the cross chain reconciliation questions before support tickets arrive.

Revolut started rolling out E U R R, a euro stablecoin, with Bridge as the regulated issuer, currently limited to selected customers in three countries. For scale, Circle's E U R C has about three hundred ninety-four and a half million euros outstanding, and E U R R is starting from close to nothing. But a neobank with tens of millions of users distributing a euro stablecoin changes the volume math quickly. Euro stablecoins are going to fragment the way dollar ones did. Our euro support should be issuer agnostic at the schema level now, while that change is still cheap.

World launched World Money, a self custodial super app that combines Bridge linked accounts with Morpho lending, plugs into Stripe and Kalshi, and gates boosted rewards behind World I D verification. Availability varies a lot by country. What interests me is that the reference architecture for consumer stablecoin apps has clearly settled: Bridge for issuance, Morpho for yield, Stripe for cards, self custody at the edge. Our treasury product competes with that same stack minus the identity layer. Also worth noting, their per jurisdiction feature gating is a product requirement, not an afterthought, and ours should be too.

Two smaller payments items. dtcpay, a Singapore stablecoin payments firm, closed a twenty-five million dollar Series A backed by Japan's S B I Group, and plans to scale its merchant network and enterprise portal. Japanese institutional money is funding A P A C stablecoin acceptance, and merchant payment service providers like this need custody and treasury underneath them. A partnership motion there is cheaper than building merchant acquiring ourselves.

And Visa is moving to close the meme coin card rewards loophole, changing how those purchases get coded so they stop counting as ordinary digital media and earning rewards. Banks lost their broader push for tighter stablecoin rules when CLARITY failed, and J P Morgan got this narrower win instead. The lesson for us is that merchant category codes are becoming a policy lever. An M C C reclassification can change interchange and decline rates on our fiat on ramp overnight. Worth asking our acquirer which code our card funding flows sit under.

Now treasury, real world assets, and corporate adoption.

The innovation exemption is the big one. A five year conditional exemption lets tokenized national market system stock trade on permissioned automated market makers, with full shareholder rights attached, and it allows third party issued tokens, not just issuer originated ones. The C F T C widened software relief the same day. The phrase that creates work for us is full rights. Holding a rights bearing token means handling dividends, splits, proxy votes and tax lots, and our current token model represents none of that. Analysts are already naming Coinbase, Robinhood and Circle as the early winners, so the window to be the neutral custodian for everyone else is narrow.

Morpho moved immediately. Holders of five Coinbase stock tokens can now pledge them for U S D C loans on Base. A treasury client can borrow dollars against equity exposure without leaving the chain, which is a genuinely useful working capital tool. Our risk engine has no equity collateral model, no market hours logic, and no trading halt handling. That is the concrete gap to scope.

On the less encouraging side, corporate treasuries bought just five thousand nine hundred bitcoin over three months, and CoinDesk notes other demand signals look weak too. Separately, VanEck criticized Metaplanet over executive dilution even after the company cut its potential share pool by forty-one percent. The bitcoin treasury company land grab is cooling. Any pipeline forecast leaning on new digital asset treasury launches needs a haircut this quarter. The revenue that holds up is servicing balance sheets that already exist, which argues for investing in reporting, audit support and governance features rather than new logo acquisition.

One more here. Ava Labs' president said N Y S E spent about a year testing Avalanche technology for its tokenization plans, describing a close working relationship with parent company I C E, though he stopped short of saying the exchange had selected Avalanche. Read that as vendor positioning rather than a signed deal. But the direction is real, and real world asset trading volume is surging on Hyperliquid, a venue we do not support. Our chain roadmap should follow tokenized asset volume rather than token market capitalization.

On to regulation and policy.

I covered the main story already, so briefly: the CLARITY Act failed to advance, and industry groups pointed straight at agency rulemaking. Several of them made the point that a rule can be rewritten by a future chair in a way a statute cannot, so this is a win with an expiry date. The practical implication for us is that our compliance architecture needs to be cheap to change. Anywhere we have hardcoded a regulatory assumption into the product, that should become configuration.

The two chairs made it explicit. C F T C chair Michael Selig said the agency is locked in and ready to ship its rules. S E C chair Paul Atkins said the commission will act decisively within its statutory authority. Both posted within two hours of each other, the day after the Senate vote. Expect a dense rulemaking calendar over the next two quarters, with real comment deadlines we can miss. Someone should own that docket calendar with named dates on it.

And on enforcement, Treasury sanctioned the Iranian exchange BitBank. O F A C says hundreds of millions of dollars in bitcoin moved through it to the Revolutionary Guards over two months, using tolls collected by the previously sanctioned Hormuz Safe platform. The relevant question for us is our screening service level agreement measured against O F A C publication time rather than our vendor's refresh cycle. I would spot check how long those new addresses took to appear in our blocklists, because that is the number a regulator will eventually ask for.

Finally, smart contracts and infrastructure.

S and P Global agreed to acquire OpenZeppelin. OpenZeppelin keeps its name and its audit team as a standalone unit, while S and P extends from financial risk assessment into onchain technology risk. OpenZeppelin says its standards secure thirty-seven trillion dollars in onchain value. Terms were not disclosed. Smart contract audits are heading toward rated, comparable scores, which is good for us if we grade well and awkward if a rating agency starts publishing opinions on contracts our clients hold. Worth asking our auditors what an S and P owned OpenZeppelin means for scope and turnaround ahead of renewal.

The most useful technical read this week was OpenZeppelin's piece on post quantum cryptography. Their argument is that treating quantum risk as a distant standards body problem is too narrow for tokenized finance, because the lattice and hash based replacements have very different performance profiles and key sizes than the elliptic curve signatures onchain finance is built on. For anyone touching our key management, that matters concretely. Larger signatures and different verification costs affect H S M capacity, M P C round complexity, and transaction fees. Crypto agility belongs in the signing architecture now, not in a migration project later. Enterprise requests for proposal are already asking about this.

Two threat items. North Korean operators running fake recruiter campaigns, tracked as WaterPlum, infected at least thirty thousand devices across more than a hundred countries and stole ten point seven million dollars, targeting developers with fake jobs at crypto, A I and N F T companies. That is an insider threat vector aimed directly at engineers who hold commit access and signing credentials. Our controls should assume a developer laptop is compromised: hardware backed signing, no long lived credentials on endpoints, and a review gate on anything touching the signing path.

Related, Chainalysis reports blockchain dead drop attacks up four hundred twenty percent. North Korean operators are using Tron, Aptos and B N B Smart Chain as redundant command and control paths, and suspected Iranian actors are encoding routing data inside bitcoin transactions. Chains our clients use are doubling as malware infrastructure. That is going to pull our transaction monitoring toward payload inspection rather than just counterparty screening, and I expect awkward questions from bank partners about whether we can see any of it in flows we custody.

Last item, and a cautionary one. Neutrl opened N U S D redemptions at fifty-one cents while Strata scheduled a forty-eight hour valuation update writing its junior tranche to zero. Senior tranche withdrawals settle at revised share values. A yield bearing dollar token settling at fifty-one cents is the whole argument for a tiered asset policy that clearly separates fiat backed stablecoins from synthetic yield products in our interface, our reporting, and our collateral haircuts. If a client sees both listed as U S D stablecoin, that becomes our problem the moment one of them breaks.

For the watch list. Number one, the S E C custody rule text emerging from White House review, and specifically how it defines possession or control. Number two, the conditions attached to Bastion's O C C approval, and whether other applicants follow with the same bundled model. Number three, which venues actually register as permissioned automated market makers under the innovation exemption, and whether any of them need third party custody. Number four, Arc mainnet adoption, because if U S D C liquidity migrates to Circle's own chain our integration priorities shift. Number five, E U R R supply growth measured against E U R C. And number six, whether Gemini's eighty percent drawdown from its I P O turns into an acquisition, which would reshape the American custody competitive set.

Quick numbers to close. Bitcoin around eighty-one thousand dollars, its best level since September fourth, after a week that opened with a selloff on four tenths of a percent August producer prices. Corporate treasuries bought five thousand nine hundred bitcoin in three months. Bitcoin exchange traded funds took four hundred thirty-three million dollars in net inflows on Friday, three hundred ten point seven million of that into Fidelity's F B T C, while ether funds snapped a four week inflow streak. Banks hold twenty-three percent of the M i C A register. And dead drop attacks are up four hundred twenty percent.

One gap worth flagging. The custody vendor feeds, Fireblocks, Anchorage and Circle's own blog, returned nothing this week, and neither did Blockworks or Rekt. So the direct competitor intelligence in this briefing is inferred from regulatory filings and general press rather than from the vendors themselves. If that keeps happening, those feeds may need fixing.

That is the week. The short version: Congress stepped back, the agencies stepped forward, and the custody perimeter is being redrawn by bank charters rather than by better key management. Talk to you next week.
