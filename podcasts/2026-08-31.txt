Welcome to your weekly Crypto Custody and Treasury briefing for the week of August thirty first, twenty twenty six. This week the feed pulled one hundred and forty nine stories across fifteen sources, and three threads run through almost all of them. Custody rulemaking finally has a clock on it. Stablecoin competition has moved from who issues the token to who distributes it. And three separate security failures this week came down to version hygiene rather than clever new attacks. Let us start with the three things that matter most, then walk through each area.

Takeaway one. The S E C custody rewrite has entered White House review. That means the qualified custodian question gets an answer in months, not years. The practical implication for us is that we need a branch plan on the roadmap now, before the text publishes. Draft two scenarios, one where the definition of a qualified custodian comes out broad and one where it comes out narrow, and pre write the control changes each one would trigger. That way, on the day the proposal drops, we are drafting a comment letter and a gap analysis rather than starting from a blank page while every competitor does the same thing.

Takeaway two. Stablecoin competition has shifted from issuance to distribution. In a single week, Visa signed two Korean bank partnerships, Revolut launched its own euro token through a third party regulated issuer, and thirty nine state banking groups announced a shared blockchain targeting twenty twenty seven. Supporting a single dollar token is no longer a feature. Treasury clients are going to ask us to hold and route multiple issuers, multiple currencies, and named chains, and that reshapes our asset listing backlog and our attestation review process.

Takeaway three. Three separate incidents this week traced back to code that was patched but not disclosed, or disclosed but not upgraded. Cosmos, Polygon, and the Rain card contract all failed on version discipline, not on novel cryptography. A dependency inventory and a client version attestation that we can show a diligence team is a cheap differentiator. And our incident runbook needs a chain halt path that assumes we learn about the bug from social media rather than from a vendor advisory.

Now let us go area by area.

First, custody and wallet infrastructure.

The headline is the S E C custody rewrite reaching White House review. The planned rule covers investment advisers and investment companies and clarifies digital asset custody. Importantly, it follows the withdrawal of a separate twenty twenty three proposal, so this is a fresh text rather than an edit of the old one. That means fewer safe assumptions about what carries over. This is the single most consequential item on our roadmap horizon. Once the text publishes, every registered investment adviser prospect we are talking to will ask the same question, which is whether we qualify. We should get segregation documentation, proof of reserves cadence, and insurance terms into a form we can hand over in an afternoon, and start the comment letter draft with legal this week.

Second, Bit Go bought N Y D I G's institutional trading arm for about forty two and a half million dollars in cash and stock, plus a fifteen million dollar earnout. The deal adds derivatives, structured products, and capital markets capability along with roughly thirty employees. N Y D I G keeps its power and data center business. Read that as a pure custodian buying its way into financing and derivatives, which is exactly the bundle argument our enterprise prospects keep raising in deals. Expect Bit Go to sell custody plus collateral mobility as one line item. We should decide whether we partner for financing or build it, and have that answer ready for the next competitive loss review.

Third, One Key's security team reproduced a transaction replacement attack against an older version of Ledger's Ethereum app. Ledger fixed the issue in version one point twenty two point two and says One Key restated existing findings rather than discovering something new. No user funds were lost. The interesting part is not the dispute, it is the underlying point that a patched device is only safe if the firmware actually got applied. If we support hardware signers anywhere in our approval flows, we should surface the signer firmware version in the interface and block signing on versions carrying known advisories, rather than trusting the client to have updated on their own.

Fourth, a card contract exploit. Card issuer Rain says an attacker used an outdated version of its Solana contract that a small number of programs were still running, draining about five hundred thousand dollars from one thousand six hundred and eighty five users. Rain and Avici both say affected users will be made whole. In the same week, a separate one point one million dollar crypto card hack knocked a neobank's token down forty nine percent. Spend programs are the softest edge of any treasury product because they require warm balances and third party contracts. If we ship or integrate a card, we should cap the hot float per program, make the sweep threshold a configurable policy rather than a hardcoded constant, and treat the issuer's contract version as a monitored dependency.

Fifth, and this one is a demand signal rather than a risk. Galaxy Research tracked bitcoin dormant for ten years or more moving at an unusual pace this year, including six ancient wallets shifting forty million dollars in a single ten day stretch this month. Most of that flow avoided exchanges entirely. Coins moving off self custody without touching an exchange usually means they are moving into a custodian. That points to demand for a high touch migration path, meaning cold to cold transfer with staged test amounts, dual approval, and provenance documentation. Worth checking whether our onboarding flow can handle a nine figure single holder migration without a manual escalation every time.

Next, stablecoins and payments.

Visa signed its second Korean stablecoin deal in one week, this time with Dunamu, the operator of Korea's largest exchange, Upbit. It follows an arrangement with Shinhan Financial days earlier. Dunamu says the partnership may explore Open Standard's O U S D token. Two deals in one week with the second and third largest Korean financial groups tells us where the next wave of corporate treasury demand originates. If we have any Asia Pacific ambition, a won denominated or O U S D compatible asset belongs on the listing roadmap, and our reserve attestation review has to handle non dollar issuers.

Revolut began rolling out its euro stablecoin, E U R R, to selected customers in three countries, with Bridge acting as the regulated issuer. Circle's E U R C remains the larger euro token by outstanding supply. The issuance as a service model means the number of euro tokens we may be asked to support is going up rather than converging. Our asset onboarding process should separate the brand from the issuer of record, because a client holding E U R R is taking Bridge risk, and our risk disclosures need to say that plainly.

Circle took Chelsea's front of shirt sponsorship, putting U S D C on the men's, women's, and academy shirts this season. It is a Premier League first for a stablecoin, and it lands shortly after the U K Financial Conduct Authority warned clubs about questionable crypto sponsorship arrangements. Circle is buying mainstream retail recognition, which raises the baseline expectation that any treasury platform supports U S D C natively and well. The less obvious point is that sponsorship money flows through club treasuries, and sports organizations are a real segment for a stablecoin denominated operating account.

Meanwhile, the Bank for International Settlements chief, Pablo Hernandez de Cos, said stablecoins lack credibility as payment instruments at scale. A companion Financial Stability Institute study highlighted how sharply issuer rules differ across jurisdictions. That second finding matters more than the quote. If issuer rules diverge by jurisdiction, a single global stablecoin policy inside our product will not hold. We need per jurisdiction asset eligibility as a configuration surface rather than a hardcoded allowlist, and our chief financial officer buyers will keep asking us for the redemption risk answer the B I S is pointing at.

On the bank side, J P Morgan is weighing its own stablecoin while large banks explore a global consortium. Separately, thirty nine state banking groups formed the industry owned BankChain Alliance targeting a twenty twenty seven launch, supporting tokenized deposits, stablecoins, smart payments, and automated settlement. The technology partner has not been selected yet. Tokenized deposits are a different asset class from stablecoins. They are a bank liability, with restricted transferability, and probably permissioned settlement. If BankChain ships in twenty twenty seven, our data model needs somewhere to put an asset that is redeemable only at the issuing bank. That is worth a spike now on whether our ledger can represent restricted transfer assets at all.

Next, treasury, real world assets, and corporate adoption.

Stellar's tokenized real world asset market more than quadrupled this year to nearly four billion dollars. A RedStone report put tokenized real world assets on Stellar above three billion dollars in July, against DeFi value it measured at two hundred and thirteen million. A fourteen to one ratio of tokenized assets to onchain trading activity describes a custody market, not a trading market. Those assets need holding, reporting, and corporate action handling far more than they need liquidity. If Stellar is not on our supported chain list, this is the quarter to price that integration.

Tokenized stock transfer volume jumped four hundred and fifteen percent in thirty days to twenty nine and a half billion dollars, with active addresses and holders more than doubling. Coinbase launched tokenized stocks on Base using the new B twenty standard, and Bybit is adding around the clock options on stock perpetuals starting September seventeenth. Tokenized equities bring problems our current asset model probably does not handle, including dividends, splits, and a transfer agent of record. Before a client asks us to hold B twenty tokens, we should know whether our accounting export can produce a cost basis and a corporate action history for them.

On the treasury vehicle side, Evernorth's registration statement for its merger with the special purpose acquisition company Armada is now effective, clearing the way for a shareholder vote on a Nasdaq listing. Its X R P position currently sits below what it paid. Separately, the French firm Capital B raised twenty four and a half million dollars for its bitcoin treasury with backing from Adam Back and TOBAM. Public treasury vehicles are still forming even with positions underwater, and each one is a custody mandate with public company reporting attached. The differentiated feature for that segment is not storage, it is auditor ready attestation on a quarterly close calendar. Worth asking sales how many of these are in the pipeline and whether our reporting cadence matches their quarterly filing timing.

Collateral lending also kept expanding. Coinbase and Better took bitcoin backed mortgages beyond the waitlist, pairing a conventional home loan with a separate downpayment loan secured by pledged bitcoin and a second lien on the property. Bullish extended a one hundred million dollar stablecoin facility to U S D dot A I for lending backed by A I computing hardware. And Russia's largest bank, Sber, plans to accept Tether and Ether as loan collateral. Pledged collateral lending is the fastest growing reason an institution needs a custodian rather than an exchange, because the lender wants a neutral third party holding the lien. Our pledge and release primitives, and specifically the speed of a margin call release, are becoming a sales requirement rather than a nice to have.

Now, regulation and policy.

The Clarity Act slipped to September, and banks are building anyway. The market structure bill missed its window, while bank infrastructure work including BankChain and the J P Morgan evaluation proceeds on its own schedule. We should stop treating the legislation date as a roadmap gate. Banks are shipping into ambiguity. Any roadmap item we have parked behind waiting on Clarity deserves a second look this planning cycle, especially since the S E C custody rule is the nearer term forcing function anyway.

An investigation found that websites of certain crypto advisors displayed S E C certificates that were never issued, carrying identifiers copied from phony F I N R A forms. If advisors are forging registrations, some of them are opening accounts somewhere. Our institutional onboarding should verify adviser registration against the S E C's public adviser register programmatically, rather than accepting a document upload. Small change, outsized compliance payoff.

And in Russia, Sber plans to take Tether and Ether alongside bitcoin as loan collateral as the country introduces regulated crypto trading under a new law. Not a market we can serve, but a useful prompt on sanctions screening. As more jurisdictions build regulated crypto collateral lending, counterparty screening on pledge and release flows needs to catch jurisdiction as well as address, and our travel rule vendor coverage should be reviewed against the new regimes.

Finally, smart contracts and infrastructure.

Cronos halted its network after a seventy five million dollar exploit of Tectonic. The attacker manipulated the price of the illiquid T O N I C token, then borrowed against the inflated collateral, in a Mango Markets style oracle attack. Crypto dot com says its app and exchange were unaffected. A halted chain is a custody problem before it is a DeFi problem. Balances freeze, withdrawals queue, and clients call. We need a documented client communication path for chain halts, and our status page should distinguish between we are down and the chain is down clearly enough that support does not have to explain the difference one conversation at a time.

Cosmos Labs said it wrongly cleared the bug behind a five point seven million dollar hack spanning six chains. MANTRA Chain, which lost three point six million, says the patch shipped only twenty hours before the attack began and did not identify the flaw it fixed. KiiChain reports losing one hundred and forty eight million tokens and says two of the three underlying defects remain unfixed upstream. A silent patch is worse than no patch, because it tells attackers exactly where to look while defenders believe nothing happened. If we run validators or full nodes on any shared framework chain, we need an upgrade service level measured in hours, and a named owner watching upstream release channels rather than waiting for release notes.

The contrast case is Polygon, which quietly patched security flaws in two hard forks before disclosing them. The Austin and Kyoto forks, deployed on the Bor and Heimdall clients, closed denial of service and validator resource risks that Polygon says were never exploited. Polygon handled this the way it should be handled, and the contrast with Cosmos is the lesson. When we assess a chain for support, coordinated disclosure track record belongs in the scorecard next to throughput and finality, because it predicts how much unplanned work that chain will cost us over a year.

Last item, Open Zeppelin shipped a real world asset wizard for Stellar, a no code interface that generates standards based contract scaffolding for representing a fund, a bond, or a private equity share as a regulated token. Open Zeppelin also partnered with Pacific Meta to bring continuous onchain security review to Japanese enterprises. Lower issuance cost means more long tail tokenized assets arriving at our door, each with its own transfer restrictions and allowlists. Standards based scaffolding is actually good news for us. If issuers converge on known templates, we can support a template once instead of reviewing every contract individually. Worth asking engineering what a template level integration would take.
