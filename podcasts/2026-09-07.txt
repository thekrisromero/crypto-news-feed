Welcome to your weekly Crypto Custody and Treasury briefing for the week of September seventh, twenty twenty six. This covers the seven days from August thirty first through September seventh. I scanned one hundred and forty five stories across nine feeds and pulled out the nineteen that actually matter if you build custody and treasury software. Let's get into it.

Here are the top three takeaways before we go deep.

Takeaway one. The S E C's digital asset custody rewrite has entered White House review. This is the rulebook we build against, and it is about to change. The planned rule covers investment advisers and investment companies, and it clarifies digital asset custody, following the withdrawal of the separate twenty twenty three safeguarding proposal. So what: reaching the Office of Management and Budget means the text is weeks away, not quarters. This is the moment to have our control narrative pre-mapped to the likely rule shape and a comment position drafted, rather than reacting after publication.

Takeaway two. Three separate custody failures landed in seven days. A three hundred and twenty million dollar drain of the Liquid federation wallet, a seven million dollar segregation gap that shut down an exchange, and a widening hardware wallet data breach. So what: proof of reserve attestation and quorum governance stop being differentiators and become table stakes in every request for proposal. Expect every enterprise deal this quarter to ask how our threshold policy prevents a single address sweep.

Takeaway three. Twenty one global banks committed to a joint dollar stablecoin the same week that Revolut and OpenReserve cleared preliminary O C C bank approvals. So what: the issuer layer is being re-intermediated by regulated banks. Our treasury product needs a multi-issuer abstraction and a bank rail settlement path, not a U S D C or U S D T binary hardcoded into the ledger.

Now let's walk through the sections.

First, custody and wallet infrastructure.

The S E C custody rewrite is the headline, and I covered the shape of it already, so let me add the practical piece. What we should be doing is inventorying which of our existing controls map to a plausible qualified custodian test. Segregation of client assets. Insolvency remoteness. Independent verification. If any of those has a gap, it becomes a roadmap priority zero item now, rather than something we discover in the middle of a client's compliance review six months from now.

Second, the Liquid sidechain. Blockstream disabled Liquid's bridge nodes after almost the entire bitcoin reserve backing L B T C, three thousand nine hundred and ninety eight bitcoin, roughly three hundred and twenty million dollars, was withdrawn to a single address. The owner of that address wrote the words "we are whitehats" into a bitcoin transaction. Exchanges suspended L B T C deposits and withdrawals while Blockstream tried to make contact. Here is the part that matters for us. A functioning federation multisignature setup still allowed a complete sweep to one destination. The failure was policy, not cryptography. The cryptography did exactly what it was told. So the question worth asking internally is whether our withdrawal policy engine can express a rule like: no single destination above some percentage of assets under custody within one time window. And whether we alert on destination concentration, not just on transaction amount. Those are different alerts and most systems only have the second one.

Third, Orionx. The Tether backed Latin American exchange is closing permanently after an audit found more than seven million dollars in customer assets had moved to wallets outside its custody. What stands out is not the amount, it is the detection method. A segregation failure of that size was caught by an annual audit rather than by continuous reconciliation. That is the gap. A continuously attested view of customer liabilities versus on chain assets is the clearest product story we can tell against exchange run custody. And it is a strong candidate for a customer visible dashboard rather than just an internal control.

Fourth, hardware wallet data. Trezor disclosed that its shipping partner ShipMonk retained records dating from twenty nineteen through twenty twenty one. Names, emails, phone numbers, shipping addresses, order numbers. That is sixty seven thousand more customers than first thought, and the retention went well beyond the ninety day window Trezor said its partners had agreed to. Separately, Ledger is facing a five hundred million dollar lawsuit over its own string of data breaches. Both of these are third party data retention failures, not key compromises. And that is exactly the risk shape our own vendor chain carries. A concrete ask for security this quarter: enumerate every subprocessor holding client identity or shipping data, and verify that retention is enforced technically, not just contractually.

Fifth in this section, quantum. The G seven urged organizations to adopt post quantum security before capable computers can compromise today's encryption and digital signatures. In parallel, a company called QuFi launched a platform that verifies digital asset transactions using post quantum cryptography without requiring changes to existing settlement networks. My read is that institutional requests for proposal will start asking for a written post quantum migration position within two or three quarters. A one page statement covering which key material is at risk, what our rotation path looks like, and where we depend on hardware security module vendor roadmaps, is cheap to write now and expensive to improvise later.

Now, stablecoins and payments.

The big one. Twenty one financial institutions have committed to a joint dollar stablecoin venture. Bank of America, Citi, Goldman Sachs, U B S, Deutsche Bank, and sixteen other firms said they will incorporate a company in the second half of twenty twenty six to issue a dollar stablecoin, with a market launch to follow. A bank consortium coin arrives with counterparties our institutional clients already face, which makes it a default rather than an option. If our asset onboarding path is a manual engineering task per issuer, that is the constraint to fix before the venture incorporates, not after it launches.

Next, Revolut started rolling out E U R R with Bridge acting as the regulated issuer. It is open to selected customers in three countries and the outstanding supply is tiny compared to Circle's E U R C, which sits near three hundred and ninety four and a half million euro. The interesting part is structural. The distributor plus licensed issuer split is becoming the standard European arrangement. Which means our reserve attestation and issuer risk model needs to represent two distinct entities per token. Who redeems and who is regulated are no longer the same answer.

Wyoming put its state stablecoin reserves on chain. The Wyoming Stable Token Commission adopted Chainlink Proof of Reserve for the Frontier Stable Token on September second. Total supply is about nine hundred and sixty eight thousand tokens, roughly zero point two percent of the five hundred and eight million the Commission has projected. Small numbers, but a U S state issuer choosing machine readable proof of reserve sets a reference standard that clients will point at. Consuming proof of reserve feeds as a first class signal in our reserve health view, rather than parsing P D F attestations, is a small build with a disproportionate trust story.

Then there is the Tether lawsuit. Two Thai businessmen are suing Tether in Manhattan federal court over forty two point four million U S D T blacklisted across ten Ethereum addresses. On chain records show all ten were frozen inside a single two and a half minute window, three months before the seizure warrant. What this tells us is that issuer freeze authority is now being litigated as a standalone risk, separate from reserve quality. Clients holding operating balances in a single stablecoin need us to surface freeze exposure explicitly. Per issuer concentration in the treasury dashboard, with an automated rebalance suggestion when it crosses a threshold.

One more. A Bank of Korea study found that dollar backed stablecoins can push local currencies lower. This arrives while Korea builds its tokenized securities framework. Expect non dollar jurisdictions to push local currency stablecoins for domestic settlement. Multi currency treasury support is a market access requirement in Asia Pacific and in Europe, not a nice to have for later.

Now, treasury, real world assets, and corporate adoption.

South Korea published a three phase roadmap to tokenize all types of securities, targeting February twenty twenty seven for its first framework. The detail that matters for us is the settlement leg. Korea ultimately wants participants to settle tokenized securities on chain using stablecoins. That makes custody of the cash leg and the security leg a single product problem rather than two. Worth a roadmap placeholder for atomic delivery versus payment support against a regulated Korean venue, given the eighteen month lead time.

The London Stock Exchange is partnering with Payward, Kraken's parent, to support x Stocks tokenized trading. In the coming weeks Payward will issue tokens for the hundred largest London listed companies. An incumbent exchange lending its name to tokenized equities changes the diligence conversation from is this legitimate to who holds it. That is a custody question. We should have a clear answer on corporate actions and dividend handling for tokenized equities before clients ask us.

Related, and messier: the A M C versus Robinhood fight. A M C's chief executive called Robinhood's stock tokens unregulated, said outside securities counsel is looking at whether it can force a halt, and used words like contemptible and vile. Robinhood's chief legal officer told him to send lawyers. A M C shares jumped twenty one percent overnight. Meanwhile Uniswap founder Hayden Adams compared Robinhood's and Ondo's stock tokens to early stablecoins, and Dinari's co-founder called the same instruments materially worse. The unresolved question underneath all the noise is whether a stock token is a claim on a custodied share or a synthetic exposure. Only the first is custodiable in the conventional sense. Our asset listing policy should require issuer level backing disclosure before we support any tokenized equity, or we inherit somebody else's legal argument.

On flows. Spot bitcoin exchange traded funds pulled in nine hundred and eighty seven million dollars last week, and three point five two billion in August, their largest positive month since September twenty twenty five. There was a seven hundred and thirty one million dollar single day, the biggest since January. Bitcoin moved between seventy seven thousand and eighty one thousand dollars as Federal Reserve expectations swung on jobs data. Sustained exchange traded fund creation means more authorized participant flow and more institutional cold storage volume upstream of us. If our onboarding service level for large first deposits is measured in days rather than hours, that is the operational bottleneck this flow will expose.

Digital asset treasury companies kept levering up. HyperStrat expanded its equity facility from one billion to two and a half billion dollars to keep accumulating H Y P E. Strategy ended its bitcoin pause and Bitmine continued its ether buying streak. Between them they now hold more than four percent of bitcoin and ether supply respectively. Treasury companies are a distinct customer archetype. Highly concentrated, publicly scrutinized, and needing auditor ready attestation on a reporting calendar. A packaged proof of holdings export for quarterly filings is an obvious wedge if we do not already have one.

Now, regulation and policy.

Revolut and a sixteen z backed OpenReserve both won preliminary O C C national bank approvals. OpenReserve is focused on on chain settlement and chose a full national charter over the trust bank route that most crypto firms have taken, which clears a path toward insured deposits and conventional lending alongside stablecoin issuance. Revolut still needs Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, Federal Reserve, and final O C C clearances before a planned twenty twenty seven launch. Full national charters bring a bundle that a pure custodian cannot match. Our competitive positioning should lean harder on independence and multi venue neutrality, because we will increasingly be compared against a bank rather than against a wallet.

Coinbase filed S E C notices seeking to list single stock perpetuals in the United States, expanding beyond thematic stock indexes, with Commodity Futures Trading Commission product approval as the stated next step. Separately the C F T C moved to dismiss C M E's lawsuit over crypto perpetual futures, calling it much ado about nothing. Regulated U S perpetuals would mean institutional clients want collateral to sit in custody and be pledged to a venue without ever leaving it. Off exchange settlement and tri party collateral mirroring move up the priority list if this clears.

On financial crime. The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network tied roughly twelve point seven to thirteen billion dollars in reported scam losses to transnational criminal organizations operating from compounds in Southeast Asia, with monthly reported sums up eighteen percent on average and the model now spreading beyond the region. The United States and the United Kingdom launched a joint alliance with parallel investigations, information sharing, and a private sector disruption operation planned in London in October. A private sector disruption operation implies information sharing requests landing on custodians. Being ready to respond to structured law enforcement requests without ad hoc engineering is worth scoping now, and clean travel rule and counterparty screening coverage is the prerequisite for that.

Two smaller policy notes. The National Sheriffs' Association dropped its consumer protection opposition to the C L A R I T Y Act and moved to neutral, saying it will let the legislative process proceed. And crypto firms urged the S E C to speed exchange traded fund reviews and allow confidential draft filings, while Jane Street and Charles Schwab pushed back over rushed launches and reduced market scrutiny. The sheriffs' shift matters because a cleared opposition lane raises the odds that U S market structure legislation actually moves this session, and that legislation would settle the custody versus broker boundary we currently hedge on.

Finally, smart contracts and infrastructure.

The most directly relevant protocol item this week is E I P seven nine zero six. It would let Ethereum accounts check what a transaction actually did, and if the outcome is malicious, reject it. That is a post execution condition check rather than a pre signature guess. For us this is significant, because it turns policy enforcement that we currently do in our own simulation layer into a chain native guarantee. Worth an engineering spike on what our policy engine could delegate if this ships, and where our simulation remains genuinely differentiated.

On chain reliability. Rollups pushed Ethereum blob usage to an all time high this week. In the same window, Robinhood Chain kept producing blocks every one hundred and one milliseconds, but its transaction data failed to reach Ethereum for exactly fourteen minutes across two gaps, and Arbitrum pointed at Ethereum's blob market. Robinhood Chain gas fees also rose eighty two fold in eleven days, topping four point four five million dollars in a single day, more than Ethereum, Solana and Tron combined. The lesson is that blob contention is now a live layer two availability risk, and a chain that looks perfectly healthy at the sequencer can be unfinalized underneath. Our confirmation policy for layer two assets should key off data availability on layer one, not sequencer block height. And blob market congestion belongs on the operations dashboard.

Last item. World open sourced ProveKit, which lets users prove identity claims like age or nationality without exposing the underlying personal data. Set against this week's two data retention breaches, selective disclosure proofs are the structural answer to holding less client personally identifiable information in the first place. Early to build on, but it is the right frame for how we talk about identity in our next compliance roadmap review.
