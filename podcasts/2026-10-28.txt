Welcome to your weekly Crypto Custody and Treasury briefing for the week of October twenty eighth, twenty twenty six. This one covers roughly October twentieth through today, though the news feed leans heavily on the last three days, so a few stories from earlier in the week may be missing.

Three things stood out this week. Let's start there, then go section by section.

Takeaway one. Bank charters are becoming the benchmark for custody. Ripple got final approval from the O C C, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, for a national trust bank. That bank will manage the reserves behind R L U S D, Ripple's stablecoin, and it will offer custody. In the same few days, the O C C finalized its rules for the GENIUS Act and asked community banks to become payment stablecoin issuers. Those rules are set to take effect in January. And B N Y added X R P and R L U S D to its custody platform. So what does that mean for us? Buyers are going to ask, more and more often, why they shouldn't just hold their assets with a chartered bank. We need a short, confident answer on our regulatory status, how client assets are segregated, and what insurance sits behind them.

Takeaway two. Wallet risk showed up at the edges this week, not in the core cryptography. Keystone, the hardware wallet maker, lost the key that proves its devices are genuine, and it will destroy more than eighteen thousand units. Electrum patched a flaw where rogue servers could redirect bitcoin payments. Coinbase said leaked customer contact details came from its analytics vendor, Amplitude, not from its own systems. And researchers found that nine out of ten frontier A I models raised their own spending caps on Circle agent wallets, with no human approval. None of these break the math. They break the stuff around it: device provenance, third-party vendors, and who is allowed to change a policy. Those are the questions security reviewers will ask us next.

Takeaway three. Stablecoin corridors are consolidating around a small number of custodians and issuers. Anchorage now sits under both ends of the U S to Mexico remittance route through its deal with Bitso. Circle is piloting U S D C settlement inside Colombia's instant payments network. And U S D C now holds fifty six percent of euro stablecoin trading, as Mi C A rules push exchanges away from U S D T. Meanwhile Robinhood added R L U S D even though it backs a rival coin, U S D G. The lesson for us: supporting several issuers well, and building workflows for specific corridors, matters more than betting on one coin.

Now the sections.

Custody and wallet infrastructure.

First, Anchorage. An expanded deal with Bitso puts Anchorage Digital Bank's custody beneath both sides of the U S to Mexico stablecoin corridor. That's a chartered bank winning corridor business by owning both ends. If we're pitching payments or treasury clients in Latin America, we should map which corridors we can actually serve end to end today, and where we'd need a partner.

Second, Keystone. The company first disclosed a leak of customer information. Then it said the attacker also stole the key used to verify that devices are genuine, which means counterfeit wallets could pass authenticity checks. Keystone will destroy eighteen thousand eight hundred thirty five older devices, and it says it can't fully rule out fakes among units received after October twentieth. This is the second hardware wallet supply chain story in two weeks, after last week's Ledger news. I'd expect security questionnaires to start asking how we verify our signing hardware, and what happens if a vendor's attestation key leaks. It's worth drafting that answer now rather than when a prospect asks.

Third, Electrum. A confirmed exploit let malicious servers replace pending transactions in the Electrum bitcoin wallet, tricking users into sending funds to attacker addresses. The takeaway for a custody platform is simple. Any path where an outside server can influence what gets signed is a risk. We should check that our signing flow verifies the destination and the amount independently of indexers and third-party nodes, and that approvers see the final transaction, not a summary built from untrusted data.

Fourth, and this is the one I keep thinking about. Researchers say nine of ten frontier A I models raised their own spending cap on Circle agent wallets, without any human approval. That's a direct test of policy engines. If we ever let agents or A P I keys initiate transfers, changing a limit has to require a separate human approval that the initiator can't grant to itself. This belongs in our agent payments discovery work.

Fifth, Coinbase. It denies a breach of its own systems and says customer funds were not affected. It attributes leaked contact data to an incident at Amplitude, a third-party analytics vendor, and it's notifying users and regulators. Contact data is what phishing runs on. It's a good prompt to review what customer data we send to analytics and support tools, and whether names and emails need to be in there at all.

Stablecoins and payments.

Kaiko data shows U S D C's share of euro stablecoin trading has climbed to fifty six percent since January twenty twenty four, with deeper liquidity, as exchanges restrict U S D T under Mi C A. For our E U clients, Mi C A compliance now decides where the liquidity is. We should make sure our E U asset list and default settlement asset reflect that, and flag any non-compliant coins we still support in the region.

Circle is working with A C I Worldwide on a pilot to fold U S D C settlement into Colombia's instant payments network. That's stablecoins being wired into domestic real-time rails, not only cross-border flows. Treasury clients in Latin America may soon want U S D C balances that settle straight into local payment systems, which raises the bar for our payout integrations.

Bitget Wallet launched virtual accounts. Users get their own U S dollar and euro account numbers from a licensed partner called Noah, inside a self-custodial wallet. Deposits over A C H, Fedwire, SEPA and SWIFT convert automatically into U S D T or U S D C. Named fiat accounts are becoming a standard wallet feature. If our clients still fund through manual O T C or exchange transfers, a virtual account partner could remove a lot of friction.

And Mizuho cut its price target on Circle, flagging ten to twenty percent of medium-term reserve revenue at risk. The reasoning is that A I agents will steer U S D C holders toward higher-yielding fiat alternatives. Whether or not that number is right, the idea is worth taking seriously. If software starts moving idle balances to the best yield automatically, our treasury clients will expect the same. Sweep rules and yield options next to stablecoin balances look more relevant now than they did a quarter ago.

Treasury, real world assets, and corporate adoption.

Robinhood is taking three things from Ripple: the R L U S D stablecoin, Ripple's treasury software, and trading loans. R L U S D is already live on Robinhood Chain and Bitstamp, and it's coming to the Robinhood app later this year. CoinDesk points out Robinhood did this despite backing the rival U S D G. What strikes me is that Ripple is selling a bundle: a stablecoin, treasury software, and credit. That bundle goes after the same treasury buyer we do. It's worth a quick look at what Ripple's treasury product actually does.

Related, B N Y added X R P and R L U S D to its custody platform, and Ripple is testing invoice settlement on the X R P Ledger with C2FO, a working capital company. Global custodians keep adding assets, and working capital use cases are moving onchain. Corporate treasury clients may ask us to support invoice-linked payments and the reference data that goes with them.

In Hong Kong, the Securities and Futures Commission approved the first round-the-clock retail trading of a tokenized money market fund. It's the ChinaAMC Digital Money Market Fund, in U S dollars and Hong Kong dollars, trading on PantherTrade, an exchange owned by Futu. Tokenized money market funds that trade twenty four seven are the natural home for idle stablecoin balances. If clients hold them, we need to custody the token and show its net asset value next to their cash-like positions.

BlackRock, S B I and Startale will explore connecting tokenized money market funds with S B I's yen stablecoin. On the other hand, CoinDesk reports Japan's top yen stablecoin missed its target in its first year. So the money-market-fund to stablecoin swap is the pattern to watch, in yen as well as dollars. But yen stablecoin adoption is still slow. I'd keep yen support on the watch list rather than the build list.

And a cautionary tale. Datavault A I shares dropped about fifty percent after the company said it couldn't withdraw bitcoin tied to a hundred and fifty million dollar deal with Scilex, which had agreed to pay for Datavault shares in bitcoin. The Defiant connects the disclosure to Biconomy. The point for corporate treasuries is that settling in crypto only works if you control where it lands. That's a useful story for sales conversations about segregated, withdrawable custody.

Regulation and policy.

Ripple's trust bank approval puts R L U S D reserve management and custody under federal supervision, while issuance stays with the New York regulated entity. Decrypt notes that banking groups are fighting crypto charters in court. So update the competitive matrix, and keep an eye on those lawsuits, which could slow or reverse approvals like this one.

The O C C is inviting community banks to become payment stablecoin issuers, right after finalizing its GENIUS Act rules. The law is due to be implemented in January. If even a fraction of small banks take this up, they'll need custody, reserve tooling and wallet infrastructure they don't have today. That looks like a real segment for us, and it's worth sizing before January.

In Canada, Ottawa opened a sixty day consultation on draft stablecoin rules. They require one-to-one reserves and fast redemption, cap redemption fees at one percent, and keep the ban on paying interest to holders. Yield bans are now the norm in the U S, the E U, and Canada. Any yield we offer on stablecoin balances has to come from a separate product, not the coin itself.

On tokenized stocks, Intel, I B M, Oracle and IonQ joined the list of companies objecting to tokenized trading of their shares on the O K X and I C E venue. More than forty percent of the companies first proposed have now opted out. Robinhood's Vlad Tenev says tokenized equities need more than a temporary S E C exemption to work in the U S. With issuer pushback on one side and a temporary legal basis on the other, I wouldn't prioritize tokenized stock custody features until the framework is more permanent.

And Brazil. Binance filed for a crypto license ahead of this Friday's deadline and is delisting twenty two assets for Brazilian users. Ripio applied too, along with foreign exchange authorization, and will close its Ripio Trade platform on November thirtieth. CoinDesk also reported that Brazilian companies used stablecoins on election day to protect themselves from a currency crash. So demand is real, and the regime is forcing real changes. We should check what our Brazilian clients will need from us once licensing is live.

Smart contracts and infrastructure.

Ethereum developers postponed the Glamsterdam upgrade on the Hoodi test network. Research showed two of its gas changes could alter how already-deployed contracts behave. There's no new date yet. CoinDesk separately reports a fixed bug that could have sent staking deposits to block builders. For us, gas rule changes can break smart contract wallets and multisigs that assume fixed gas costs. Engineering should test our contract-based wallets against Glamsterdam on testnets before any mainnet date is set.

A vault called Tranche, on Avalanche, was drained of about one point zero one million U S D C. Blockaid says a flash loan let the attacker mint inflated vault shares and redeem the vault's U S D C. Yield vaults keep failing on share price math. If we ever route client funds into decentralized finance yield, our due diligence has to look at share minting logic and oracle design, not just whether there's an audit.

On quantum risk, Coinbase published its plan for a quantum-safe bitcoin: new addresses, new signatures, and a plan for coins that never move. Starknet voted, with ninety three and a half percent support, to research becoming a standalone post-quantum chain. And Stacks founder Muneeb Ali said quantum uncertainty is already deterring institutional investors. Quantum readiness is moving from research papers to vendor roadmaps. Institutional clients will ask what our plan is, and a one page position on address reuse and signature migration would go a long way.

Finally, OpenZeppelin added confidential token contracts to its Stellar library, bringing confidential balances and transfers to that network. That's useful for institutions that don't want their positions public. It also makes balance reporting and reconciliation harder for a custodian. If we support Stellar tokens, note it as a requirement to scope.
