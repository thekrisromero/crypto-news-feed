SECTION ONE: CUSTODY AND WALLET INFRASTRUCTURE

Let's start with custody, which is, of course, the core of your platform.

The biggest story in this category comes from Coinbase. The company published a detailed quantum threat report that flags exchange cold wallets as among the Bitcoin addresses most exposed to future quantum computing attacks. The specific risk is address reuse: any Bitcoin address that has already signed and broadcast a transaction has an exposed public key. A sufficiently powerful quantum computer — which doesn't exist yet, but is increasingly within a 10-to-20-year planning horizon — could derive the private key from that public key. The report proposes solutions including migration deadlines and the potential freezing of non-migrated vulnerable coins.

So what does this mean for your product? Coinbase is the first major custodian to publicly quantify their quantum exposure. Which means enterprise clients will start asking you the same question — probably in R F Ps within the next twelve months. Your action item is to audit which wallet address cohorts in your custody stack have exposed public keys, and to begin drafting a migration plan before this becomes a due-diligence liability.

On the Ethereum side, researchers floated a remarkably cheap solution: post-quantum signature verification using the SPHINCS-plus scheme could be added to Ethereum wallets for under seven cents per account. No hard fork required. This proposal is still at the research stage, but if it moves toward an E I P and gains consensus, it means E V M-based custody infrastructure could get quantum resistance nearly for free. Track this and assess which of your clients' E V M holdings would be in scope.

Also this week, OpenZeppelin announced a partnership to accelerate secure development for institutions building on Canton — the privacy-preserving blockchain used by Goldman Sachs, B N P Paribas, and Deutsche Bank for tokenized asset settlement. The financial system is moving on-chain, and Canton appears to be the settlement layer of choice for Tier One banks. If your custody stack doesn't have a Canton integration story, you risk being excluded from the tokenized asset settlement workflows that major banks are building right now.

Finally, Anchorage Digital's Head of Research walked through how institutional clients — including E T Fs, treasury companies, miners, and crypto-native desks — are using covered calls and other options strategies to generate yield on idle Bitcoin holdings in custody. The takeaway here is that custody is no longer just safeguarding. Clients expect yield-generating programs on assets that would otherwise sit idle. If you aren't already offering or facilitating options or lending programs, Anchorage and its competitors are already having this conversation with your target accounts.

SECTION FOUR: REGULATION AND POLICY

The regulatory picture this week was dominated by a single major development, but there were several other signals worth tracking.

The S E C proposed on June 11 to rescind Rule 611 and Rule 610(e) from Regulation N M S — opening a 60-day comment period. Rule 611 is the "trade-through rule" that requires broker-dealers to route orders to the venue displaying the best automated quote. Rule 610(e) restricts access fees. Together, these rules have been a structural barrier to tokenized stocks trading through D e F i Automated Market Makers, because D e F i venues can't satisfy traditional best-execution routing requirements. Removing them would let tokenized equities trade through A M Ms without those constraints — a major unlock for on-chain equity markets. Ledger Insights notes that the S E C's proposal isn't likely to survive a full rulemaking process without challenge, but the direction of intent is clear. File a comment, brief your legal team, and begin roadmapping what qualified custodian support for tokenized equities looks like under the proposed framework.

Japan made parallel moves this week. The country's lower house passed a bill amending the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act to regulate crypto like stocks — opening a path to regulated crypto E T Fs by 2027 and cutting the capital-gains tax from up to 55 percent to a flat 20 percent. The upper house vote is still pending. Japan's retail crypto market is one of the largest in the world, and regulated E T F custody could be a significant new revenue line for platforms already licensed there.

South Korea's finance ministry also weighed in, saying tokenized stocks are securities — not crypto assets — which would make them subject to taxes as early as the second half of 2026 if financial regulators agree. Combined with the S E C's N M S proposals and Japan's F I E A bill, there's a clear global pattern: tokenized securities are being treated as securities, with all the regulatory obligations that implies for custodians.

On the personnel side, S E C Commissioner Hester Peirce, known industry-wide as "Crypto Mom," departed the S E C this week after nearly 30 years. Peirce had been the most consistently pro-crypto voice at the commission, and her absence removes a reliable advocate for industry-friendly rulemaking. Monitor who the White House nominates to fill her seat — that appointment will meaningfully shape the S E C's posture on custody reform, E T F approvals, and tokenized securities going forward.

Finally, Coin Center filed two substantive comment letters to FinCEN this week — one on updated A M L / C F T program requirements, and one specifically on P P S I, or Primary Payment System Infrastructure, sanctions program requirements. Coin Center is pushing back against surveillance mandates that would capture non-custodial wallets and privacy-enhancing tools. The final FinCEN rule will directly shape your Travel Rule and transaction monitoring obligations. If the P P S I rules are adopted broadly, you may face new requirements for monitoring transactions involving privacy-preserving protocols on chains your clients currently use.

If your team hasn't run phishing simulations specifically targeting wire and withdrawal approval flows, that's your most urgent security investment.
