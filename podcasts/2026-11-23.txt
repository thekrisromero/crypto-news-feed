Welcome to your weekly Crypto Custody and Treasury briefing for the week of November twenty third, twenty twenty six. This episode covers news from November sixteenth through today. Takeaway number one. Stolen keys did more damage this week than broken smart contracts. Chainflip, the cross chain swap protocol, lost about twelve million dollars in what The Block describes as a suspected key compromise. Fluidkey published a post mortem saying stolen session tokens let an attacker stage transfers that users' own apps then signed. The so what for us: our pitch rests on key management and policy controls, so clients are going to ask how we scope A P I keys, how we expire sessions, and how we verify hardware provenance. We should have those answers written down before the questions arrive.

Takeaway number two. Banks are building tokenized cash rails, and corporate treasury flows will follow them. The European Central Bank opened applications for its first Appia experiments on tokenized deposits and securities, with a deadline of December eleventh. Ant International, D B S, and Franklin Templeton launched a protocol that automatically switches between tokenized bank deposits and tokenized money market funds. And M U F G described a test on Canton for intraday Japanese government bond repo funding. The so what: treasury clients will want tokenized deposits and tokenized money market funds held next to their stablecoins, with automatic sweep rules between them. Canton support and sweep policies should be part of the next roadmap conversation.

B T C S, an ether treasury company, told the S E C that its fifty four million dollar loan on Aave could be liquidated.
