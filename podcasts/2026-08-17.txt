Welcome to your weekly Crypto Custody and Treasury briefing for the week of August seventeenth, twenty twenty six. I pulled one hundred twenty nine stories from the feeds and picked out the ones that actually matter to a product manager working on custody and treasury. Let's get into it.

Three things first, if you only listen to the top of this.

One. Regulators are shipping custody rules faster than Congress is shipping law. S E C staff cleared Franklin Templeton's registered funds to hold an onchain money fund as cash and as collateral, attaching twelve explicit custody and control conditions. The same week, the O C C handed Trump backed World Liberty Financial a conditional national trust charter. Meanwhile Galaxy cut the odds of the CLARITY Act passing to ten percent. So what: our regulatory roadmap should stop waiting on statute and start building against charter conditions and staff level relief, because that is where the operative requirements are being written.

Two. Self custody had its worst week in years, and the damage reached vendors, not just wallets. Galaxy now estimates thefts linked to compromised Coldcard hardware wallets could top one hundred fifty million dollars. Trezor and SafePal between them leaked personal data on roughly fifty four thousand customers. So what: this is the cleanest qualified custody sales trigger we will get all quarter, and it is also an instruction to audit our own third party vendors, because neither breach touched a private key.

Three. Tokenized collateral crossed from pilot to plumbing. Franklin's onchain money fund was cleared for fund cash and collateral, Bitwise is exploring tokenizing its Solana staking E T F through Superstate, and tokenized equity transfer volume jumped one hundred seventy nine percent to twenty three point one three billion dollars across one point three one million holders. So what: treasury clients will start asking us to hold and mobilize tokenized money market shares as collateral, and we need a roadmap answer on transfer agent integration and net asset value sourcing before an R F P forces one.

Now the sections. Starting with custody and wallet infrastructure.

The benchmark story is BitGo. It posted a nineteen million dollar net loss in the second quarter and its chief financial officer is leaving. Revenue was up eighty percent year over year, but trading and staking margins weakened, and the company is targeting fifteen million dollars in annualized savings after June layoffs. Mizuho cut its price target to eleven dollars while still calling it a high growth recurring revenue business, citing twenty seven percent year over year customer growth. So what: our closest listed comparable is proving that custody revenue growth does not automatically carry margin. We should pull our own unit economics on trading and staking spread versus pure custody fees before the next pricing review, because that customer count number is what our board will anchor on.

Next, the O C C granted conditional approval for World Liberty Financial to become a national trust bank. The proposed World Liberty Trust Company would take over issuance of the U S D one stablecoin from BitGo, which handles it today. Two lessons there. First, an O C C national trust charter is a live and reasonably fast route to qualified custodian status, and deserves a real cost and timeline estimate from us. Second, and less comfortable: issuer clients will bring custody in house once they hold their own charter, so stablecoin reserve custody revenue should be modelled as a stepping stone, not an annuity.

On self custody, Galaxy Research says Coldcard linked thefts have slowed, which they read as vulnerable holders having either migrated or already been drained, with total losses possibly above one hundred fifty million dollars. The same week featured a single wallet exploit worth one hundred sixteen million. So what: ask marketing for a one page comparison of our key generation ceremony, attestation, and insurance posture against a consumer hardware wallet, and get it into sales hands before the next enterprise cycle.

More instructive are the two wallet data breaches. A breach at Trezor's shipping partner ShipMonk exposed names and contact details for thirteen thousand six hundred eighty nine customers across seven countries who ordered between May tenth and August eighth. Trezor says its devices and systems were not compromised. Separately, SafePal disclosed a breach affecting nearly forty thousand customers' order information, with phishing reports going back to July. Neither breach touched a key. Both touched a vendor. So what: our third party risk register needs a fresh pass over fulfilment, customer relationship management, support desk, and marketing automation vendors, because a client list leak from any of them creates targeted phishing against exactly the accounts with the largest balances.

Finally, Bank Leumi, Israel's largest bank, will offer crypto trading on Galaxy's custody stack from early twenty twenty seven, retrying after a twenty twenty two attempt died at the Bank of Israel. Customers will buy, hold, and sell Bitcoin, Ether, and Solana inside the bank's investment app. So what: the bank distribution deal is becoming the standard custody go to market, and Galaxy is winning them. Worth an honest check on whether our sub custody interface, per customer segregation reporting, and local regulatory reporting hooks could be white labelled by a tier one bank today, or whether that is a six month integration.

Moving to stablecoins and payments.

Tether cleared its first full audit from K P M G, an unqualified opinion on twenty twenty five accounts for its El Salvador issuing entity. Two details matter more than the headline. The work was done to A I C P A standards rather than the P C A O B standards the GENIUS Act sets for large issuers, and Tether has not published the statements, citing its status as a private company. So what: if our platform surfaces reserve attestations to treasury clients, we should label which standard each issuer was audited against rather than showing a generic green tick, because that is exactly what a client's own auditor will ask about.

Standard Chartered's Anchorpoint venture launched the beta of its H K D A P stablecoin alongside licensed Hong Kong exchanges HashKey and O S L, under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority's regime. So what: non dollar bank issued stablecoins are moving from announcement to live issuance. If our asset listing pipeline is still dollar first, Hong Kong dollars and other licensed regional units need a place in the queue, because Asia Pacific treasury clients will want settlement balances in their own currency.

Tempo launched an embedded yield product for platforms, starting with Deel. Tempo Earn routes rewards through Morpho vaults and tokenized money market funds, deliberately placing the yield outside the stablecoin issuer, which United States law bars from paying interest. So what: this is the emerging template for legally paying yield on stablecoin balances. Our treasury product needs a view on whether we intermediate that structure, custody the vault positions, or stay out, because telling a client the issuer cannot pay them is no longer a sufficient answer.

That connects to the broader fight. The stablecoin yield clash between banks and crypto is not going away, with deposit economics on one side and the growth case on the other, and it was one of the unresolved issues Galaxy cited when cutting CLARITY odds. So what: the outcome sets the ceiling on our treasury product's headline rate, so build the roadmap with a pluggable yield source, whether that is an issuer rebate, a tokenized money fund, or a vault.

One smaller item. Stablecoin card issuer Rain acquired Ansa, which builds branded prepaid wallets holding dollar balances for merchants with no stablecoin leg. So what: card issuers buying into stored value means accumulating float that needs custody and reconciliation, and that is a buyer persona we do not usually pitch.

Now treasury, real world assets, and corporate adoption.

The most product relevant item of the week is the S E C staff relief letting Franklin Templeton's registered funds hold F O B X X, its onchain money fund, through an affiliated blockchain integrated system, as cash and as collateral, subject to twelve custody and control conditions. So what: those twelve conditions are effectively a draft specification for what S E C staff considers adequate control over tokenized fund shares. Someone should read them line by line and map each to a control we already have, one we can build, or a real gap. That map is the fastest route to a defensible tokenized collateral offering.

In the same vein, the S E C advanced a tokenized securities exemption that could enable twenty four seven trading, with Chair Atkins saying it would support compliant onchain trading while longer term rules develop. So what: continuous trading breaks assumptions baked into most custody operating models, including cut off times, end of day reconciliation, and settlement window staffing. An early engineering spike on what a twenty four seven posture would cost us is worth funding.

A reality check on tokenization economics. Securitize's tokenized assets hit four point three billion dollars, but tokenization revenue dropped twelve percent while operating costs jumped fifty six percent. So what: assets under administration are growing faster than the ability to monetize them, which argues for us monetizing tokenized assets through custody and collateral servicing rather than through issuance.

Demand, though, is real. Tokenized equities reached one point three one million holders over the past month, with monthly transfer volume up one hundred seventy nine percent to twenty three point one three billion dollars, and distributed value up almost six percent. So what: transfer volume growing far faster than holder value means high velocity trading, not buy and hold. Our fee model should assume transaction heavy usage, and reconciliation load will scale with transfers rather than balances.

On the allocator side, Norway's sovereign wealth fund saw indirect Bitcoin exposure hit an all time high, with Strategy accounting for eighty six percent of it, per K thirty three. It also disclosed about eighty two million dollars in the Ethereum treasury firm BitMine. So what: the largest allocators are still getting exposure through listed proxies rather than direct custody, and that gap is our addressable market. The pitch is not own crypto. The pitch is replace eighty six percent single name concentration risk with a directly custodied position.

That thesis got a boost from M S C I, which proposed a non operating company screen that could remove Strategy and Metaplanet from its global indexes. Strategy responded that M S C I should measure markets, not dictate corporate asset allocation. So what: if index exclusion raises the cost of the treasury company wrapper, some demand plausibly shifts toward direct corporate holdings with a qualified custodian. Flag it to sales with a trigger date once the consultation concludes.

And Bitwise is exploring tokenizing its Solana staking E T F through Superstate. Shares held in tokenized form would carry the same rights as book entry shares but would not be freely transferable outside the system. So what: a tokenized fund share gated by a permissioned transfer system is a custody product, not a decentralized finance one, and it is worth confirming our ledger can represent that state.

On to regulation and policy.

Galaxy cut CLARITY Act odds to ten percent, citing unresolved issues around ethics, stablecoin yield, and developer protections, plus a narrow Senate window in September. So what: plan for no market structure statute this Congress. Build the compliance roadmap on hooks that already exist, namely trust charters, state licensing, and S E C staff positions, and re justify any roadmap item whose business case depends on CLARITY passing.

Days after the Senate punted, the S E C shelved its own crypto rulemaking meeting citing an unforeseen scheduling issue, with no replacement date. The agenda had included a tokenized securities offering proposal. So what: both the legislative and rulemaking tracks stalled in the same week, leaving staff level relief as the only one still moving. Any customer facing timeline that assumes a formal S E C rule this year should be revised before somebody quotes it externally.

The Blockchain Association is urging the Supreme Court to take up Custodia's case, arguing broad Federal Reserve discretion over master accounts could push crypto firms out of the banking system. So what: master account access is the difference between holding client fiat directly and renting a correspondent relationship. If the Court declines, our banking partner concentration risk becomes permanent and should carry a named backup partner.

Ireland published its first anti money laundering strategy of this kind, with enhanced due diligence for transfers involving private wallets and for firms dealing with overseas crypto companies. So what: self hosted wallet withdrawals are being screened more tightly across the European Union. If proof of address ownership and counterparty attestation is a manual compliance step for us today, that is a scaling problem and deserves a ticket.

A second order effect worth knowing: the delisting wave following M i C A enforcement has created openings for scams targeting displaced users across the European Union. So what: forced migration makes users easy to phish, because an attacker can credibly impersonate a migration notice. Any onboarding of displaced European users needs out of band verification from the first email.

Last section, smart contracts and infrastructure.

An attacker drained ninety nine point seven percent of the Coreum bridge's X R P reserve by creating fake deposit transactions that never occurred on the source chain. So what: deposit crediting logic that trusts an unverified source chain event is the same failure mode that hits custodians, not just bridges. Confirm our deposit finality rules require independent confirmation depth per chain rather than relying on a single indexer or node provider.

Marinade reported twenty eight point eight three percent of Solana stake went delinquent after a twelve site routing failure at Teraswitch, with ninety four percent of the one hundred eighteen point eight nine million S O L on its autonomous system going dark. That came close to a finality halt. So what: one hosting provider nearly stalled finality on a chain we custody and stake. If our validators sit with one or two providers or in a single region, that is a client facing availability risk we will be asked about in due diligence.

Ethereum core developers are narrowing the sixty six proposals attached to the Hegotá upgrade, which aims to bring more native privacy to Ethereum applications. So what: protocol level privacy complicates transaction monitoring, travel rule attribution, and proof of reserve visibility. Better to have compliance and engineering reading the shortlist now than to discover after the fork that a screening vendor cannot see what it used to.

And Circle's wrapped Bitcoin product, cirBTC, went live on Ethereum with only forty Bitcoin outstanding, with Circle National Trust custodying the backing and Chainlink Proof of Reserve providing onchain visibility. So what: a chartered trust company, published addresses, and a proof of reserve oracle is becoming the expected transparency package for wrapped assets, and it is worth checking what we can produce today without new engineering.

The watch list. Whether the S E C reschedules its shelved tokenized securities meeting. The September Senate window for the CLARITY Act. Whether the Supreme Court takes Custodia's master account case. Publication of the O C C's conditions on World Liberty's trust charter, which will be the clearest public template yet for what a crypto national trust bank must commit to. M S C I's conclusion on the non operating company screen. The Hegotá privacy proposal shortlist. And BitGo's cost reduction execution, the cleanest public read on custody margin pressure.
